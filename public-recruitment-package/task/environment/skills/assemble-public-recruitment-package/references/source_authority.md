# Source Authority and Eligibility

Use this reference when records overlap, disagree, use governed terminology, or have different business roles.

## Decide authority at claim level

Authority attaches to a decision, object, scope, and period. It does not attach to a file as a whole.

For each claim:

1. State the exact decision being made.
2. Identify the affected role, cohort, stage, or endpoint.
3. Record the source that is authorized to decide that kind of claim.
4. Check its scope and effective period.
5. Record supersession, corroboration, or conflict separately.
6. Mark the claim closed only when one applicable authority supports the published status.

Binding law and regulation control within their scope. Regulator, licensing, accreditation, and collective agreement rules follow within their delegated scope. A current commissioning decision controls local choices left open by those sources. A role brief controls the duties and conditions it is authorized to adopt. An authoritative domain source controls its terminology, hierarchy, equivalence, and review route. A template controls presentation only while adopted. An individual record describes one person.

If two applicable sources conflict and neither clearly supersedes the other, do not average or blend them. Put the claim in the issue register with the competing text, affected outputs, decision owner, and release impact.

## Reconstruct eligibility logic

Break prose into atoms and record the operator that connects them.

- Mandatory atoms joined by AND are all required.
- Alternative routes joined by OR are independently sufficient.
- Conditions inside one route stay inside that route.
- Preferences rank or inform; they do not exclude unless separately authorized.
- An exclusion needs affirmative authority and a defined object.
- An exception needs a trigger, reviewer, evidence basis, and resulting action.

Verify the reconstructed expression by route testing. Create one hypothetical applicant who satisfies each valid route while failing the other routes. Each should still pass the route expression. Then fail one mandatory atom in each route and confirm the result changes. This catches migrated conditions and collapsed alternatives.

Counterexample: if Route A requires a credential and experience, while Route B requires a license, rewriting the rule as credential AND experience AND license destroys the alternatives. Rewriting it as credential OR experience OR license makes partial fragments sufficient. Both are logically wrong.

## Apply governed vocabularies

When eligibility refers to a directory, taxonomy, classification, license list, or equivalence standard:

1. Identify the authoritative source and version or effective period.
2. Record the required hierarchy level, such as family, category, specialty, or exact entry.
3. Bind the lookup to the affected role and eligibility atom.
4. Match the candidate term at the required level, including authorized aliases or renamed entries.
5. Preserve the official procedure for ambiguous, renamed, or unlisted entries.
6. Distinguish interpretation from final eligibility approval.

Before matching, identify which evidence establishes the applicant's term. Use the credential, license, transcript, registry entry, or other review document named by the governing rule. A resume title, employer label, or self-description is not a substitute unless the authority expressly makes it one. Record both the evidence type and the field from which the controlled term is read.

Test hierarchy direction explicitly. When a requirement names an exact leaf entry, membership in its broader parent does not satisfy the leaf. When it names a parent category, a descendant is eligible only if the governing source makes descendants members of that category and no authorized exclusion narrows the set. Never turn a few familiar descendants into an exhaustive list unless the authority does so.

For an ambiguous, renamed, or unlisted entry, publish or retain a review rule with four closed parts: the condition that triggers review, the authorized reviewer, the evidence basis, and a nonautomatic outcome. Suitable evidence may include a transcript, curriculum, main-course record, licensing history, or another source authorized for that taxonomy. A rule that promises acceptance, declares rejection, or names a reviewer without an evidence basis is not closed.

Keep three time questions separate:

- Which source version governs?
- During which period must the applicant hold or obtain the evidence?
- At which stage is the evidence checked?

Combining these boundaries can silently create a new restriction.

## Control personal evidence

For each personal record, state whose fact it proves and for what authorized decision. Use it only for that object. A resume may support an applicant's experience review but cannot establish the employer's general experience threshold. A private contact detail cannot become the public enquiry channel without express authority.

## Verification ledger

For each material claim, retain:

| Field | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Claim and object | Exact controlled proposition |
| Authority | Source and authorized decision type |
| Scope and period | Applicability boundary |
| Logic | Atom, operator, qualifier, and route |
| Domain interpretation | Taxonomy level and match method |
| Review path | Owner and evidence for unresolved cases |
| Destinations | Every output that repeats the claim |
| Status | Closed, pending, conflicting, or inaccessible |

The method is complete when another reviewer can reproduce both the source choice and the logical result without relying on the final prose.
