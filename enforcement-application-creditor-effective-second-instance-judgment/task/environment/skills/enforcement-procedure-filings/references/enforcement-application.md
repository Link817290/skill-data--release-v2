# Applying to the enforcement organ

Apply this reference when the deliverable asks an enforcement organ to start, resume or extend
compulsory enforcement against an obligor under a title that has taken effect.

## 1. What this document is, and what it is not

It is an application, not a pleading. Nobody is on the other side of it when it is filed, there is no
issue to be decided, and there is nothing to persuade anyone of. Its reader is the officer who will
open the case and then act on what it says. That gives it three properties:

- **No contested reasoning.** It has no facts-and-reasons part, no argument that the claim is well
  founded, no rebuttal. The merits were decided in the title.
- **Its body is a list.** The operative part of the document is an enumerated list of what is applied
  for, and almost all of the document's value sits there.
- **Preconditions are stated, not argued.** Before the list, one short passage establishes that the
  title exists, that it has taken effect, and that the time for voluntary performance has passed
  without performance. That passage is the whole of the "why now".

## 2. Structure

1. Title of the document.
2. Party block: the applying party first, the party against whom enforcement is sought second, each in
   its enforcement designation, as `enforcement-filing-baseline.md` sections 2 and 3 set out.
3. The opening passage: the title, identified by issuing body, date and case number for each decision
   that contributes to it; the fact and date of its taking effect; the period the title allowed for
   performance and the fact that it expired without performance.
4. The applied-for list, under its own heading, numbered.
5. A short note fixing the provisional cut-off date and stating the day from which the consequences of
   non-performance run, where the list uses either.
6. Sign-off, with any schedule named as an attachment.

Nothing else. In particular: no chronology of the underlying dispute, no summary of the reasons of the
decision, no section on jurisdiction, and no statement of what the applicant intends to do next.

## 3. Building the applied-for list

One numbered item per limb of the title's operative part, in the title's own order. Each item names the
obligor, the act required, and the sum or the object, in the form
`enforcement-title-and-reach.md` section 3 requires.

- **Transcribe the title's own wording for fixed sums and for formulas.** Where the title fixes a base,
  a period and a rate standard, those three travel together into the item unchanged, including the
  title's segmentation where a base changes partway through a period. Recomputing or simplifying a
  formula the title fixed is an error even when the arithmetic is right: the officer enforces the
  title, and a paraphrase invites a dispute about whether it matches.
- **Costs and expenses of the concluded proceedings** enter the list only to the extent the title puts
  them on this obligor. Where the title apportions them, take this obligor's share and leave the rest.
  Where a stage's costs were put on your own client, they never enter the list.
- **Keep a running item's ends visible.** A running amount that continues until payment is written as
  running until payment, and a provisional figure computed to a cut-off date is labelled as
  provisional. Name the cut-off date when the applicant has selected it; otherwise use one clear
  placeholder for the still-open cut-off/application date. A running amount presented as a final figure is wrong in
  a way the officer will act on.
- **The additional amount for the period of delayed performance** is its own item, not a rider on
  another. Three things fix it and all three go in the text: its base is the money obligations the
  title imposed, taken without the ordinary interest that already runs on them; its starting day is
  the day after the period for performance expired; and its rate is the standard the procedural rules
  and their implementing interpretation fix for it, cited as that standard. Write the base out so it
  can be reconciled with the items it is built from, and do not state the rate as a figure you cannot
  place; name the standard instead.
- **Extensions.** An item the title did not award, such as a further sum said to follow in law from
  the obligor's failure to pay something the title did award, may be included where the client wants
  it, but the item must stand on its own stated basis and must not be written as though the title had
  awarded it. Where such an item is included, it is the one item in the list that carries a reason.
  Where it is left out, nothing is lost: another practitioner's list may legitimately omit it.
- **A provisional overall total** is optional. If given, it is labelled provisional, it names or clearly
  marks the open cut-off date, and it reconciles with the items above it. If any component is open, it inherits that: see
  `unsupported-specifics-and-placeholders.md` section 3.

Verification: read the list as an officer. For each item, could you act on it today without asking the
applicant a question? An item that fails that test is either underspecified or belongs in the notes.

## 4. The preconditions passage

Three assertions, in this order, in as few sentences as they take:

1. The title: every decision that contributes, each with its issuing body, its date and its case
   number, in instance order.
2. That it has taken effect, with the procedural event and the date that made it so.
3. That the period for performance the title fixed has expired and the obligor has not performed.

The third is what entitles the applicant to apply, and it is an assertion of fact about the obligor's
conduct. State it flatly. Do not soften it, do not describe negotiations, and do not recount demands
unless the title made a demand a precondition.

## 5. Timeliness

An application to enforce is itself subject to a window, running from the last day of the period the
title allowed for performance. Compute it as part of the date chain
(`enforcement-title-and-reach.md` section 4) and check that the application's own date falls inside it.
Where it plainly does, the document does not argue the point: the dates in the preconditions passage
already show it. Where the window is close or the starting event is contested, state the starting
event expressly so the reader can check.

## 6. Assets of the obligor

Where the applicant holds leads on property, accounts or receivables, they go in their own part after
the list, or in a named schedule, each lead identified and each with its source.

Where the applicant holds none, the application says nothing about assets. Do not speculate about what
the obligor may own, do not list categories of property in the abstract, and do not ask the organ, in
this document, to investigate. A lead the applicant does not have is a Class B field only if the genre
and the instruction require the slot; otherwise the part simply does not exist.

## 7. Schedules

Where the computation behind a running item is long, the body may refer to a schedule by name and the
sign-off may list it as an attachment, without the schedule being reproduced inside the document. This
is the genre's own convention and is not an omission. What the body may not do is refer to a schedule
in place of stating the base, the period and the rate standard: those stay in the item.

## 8. Stop conditions

- **A person you cannot tie to a limb of the operative part** does not go in the party block, whatever
  the client says. Say so in your notes and to the client; do not put it in the document.
- **A sum the title did not fix and that you cannot state a basis for** is not an item. It is not made
  into one by marking it open.
- **Where the title's own formula is internally inconsistent**, transcribe it as it stands and record
  the inconsistency in the notes. Correcting the title inside an application is not available to you.
- **Where the inputs do not show that the title took effect**, the application cannot be made on that
  material. Say so to the client rather than asserting effect in the document.

## 9. Self-check

1. The document has no reasoning part and argues nothing, apart from any single extension item that
   carries its own basis.
2. Every item traces to a limb of the operative part, or is a marked extension with a stated basis.
3. Every fixed sum and every formula matches the title word for word, including segmentation.
4. The additional amount for delayed performance carries its base, its starting day and its named rate
   standard, and its base reconciles with the items it is built from.
5. Costs items carry only this obligor's share.
6. Every provisional figure is labelled provisional and names its cut-off date, and every cut-off date
   in the document is the same day.
7. The preconditions passage names every contributing decision, the event that gave effect, and the
   expiry of the performance period.
8. The application's own date falls inside the window for applying.
9. The body addressed is the one `enforcement-title-and-reach.md` section 5 fixes for starting
   enforcement.
