# The enforcement title: its reach, its object, its forum and its dates

Every filing in this family is bounded by an instrument that already exists. This reference is how you
establish what that instrument reaches, how you identify what enforcement is to bite on, which body
receives the filing, and how the dates are derived. Work all five sections into notes before drafting.

## 1. Identify the title, and separate what each document contributes

The enforcement title is the instrument that authorises compulsory enforcement. It may be a decision
that took effect, a ruling made inside an enforcement case, or a combination of documents.

Where more than one decision is in the inputs, two different things are being supplied and they are
usually in different documents:

- **The duty content.** Which operative part actually states what is owed or what must be done. Where
  a later instance leaves the earlier decision standing rather than replacing it, the duty content
  stays in the earlier operative part.
- **The effect.** Which decision made the duty enforceable, and on what date. That is a function of
  the instance and of the procedural event, not of which document is longest or most recent in the
  bundle.

Write both down. The filing names both, in instance order, each with its issuing body, its date and
its case number. Naming only the later decision hides where the duty comes from; naming only the
earlier one leaves the duty unenforceable on the face of the filing.

Where a ruling made inside the enforcement case is what the filing attacks or defends, that ruling is
identified by its own case number and its issuing body, and that identification appears in the
operative part of the filing, not only in the narrative.

## 2. The persons side of the reach

**Only a person on whom the operative part lays a duty may be made subject to enforcement.** Everyone
else is out, however prominent they are in the file. Work the exclusions explicitly, because the
inputs are organised to make inclusion look natural:

- A party against whom the claim was dismissed, in whole or in part, carries no duty for the
  dismissed part. A single closing limb that disposes of "the remaining claims" can be the only place
  this is stated, and it is decisive.
- A person named only in the reasons, or discussed as a related entity, an affiliate, a shareholder or
  a guarantor, carries no duty unless the operative part imposes one.
- A decision that rejects a challenge and leaves the decision below standing adds no one. It confirms
  the persons side; it never widens it.
- A person who bore costs of a stage that concerned only itself is not thereby made subject to
  enforcement for that stage.

Verification: for each person you name, quote to yourself the words of the operative part that impose
the duty. If you cannot, that person comes out, whatever the client instructs.

This is the judgement that most often decides whether the filing is usable, and it is also the one
that looks least like work. Copying the party list across is fast and wrong.

## 3. The content side of the reach, and extensions

Go through the operative part limb by limb. One row per limb, each row carrying: what is to be done,
by whom, in respect of what, and the sum or the formula the limb fixes.

Keep out of the rows:

- limbs that refused or dismissed something;
- matters the decision expressly left to a later determination;
- grievances the decision never addressed;
- anything from the reasons that the operative part did not carry through.

Anything the client wants that is not a row is an **extension**: a consequence that may follow in law
but that this title did not award. Extensions are legitimate to raise in some filings and not in
others; the route reference decides. What is never legitimate is presenting an extension as though the
title awarded it. In your notes, keep the extensions in a separate list with, for each, the reason it
is not in the title and the basis on which it is nonetheless claimed.

**Identifying what enforcement bites on.** Every row has to name its object in a form that admits of no
second candidate:

- **Immovable property**: the registered holder, the location, and whatever internal identifier makes
  the unit unique in the register. The test is whether an officer could seize or release exactly one
  thing from what you wrote.
- **A fixed sum**: the figure, in the same denomination the title uses.
- **A running sum**: the base, the period with both of its ends, and the standard that fixes the rate.
  A running sum with no base is not an item, and a period with an unstated end is written as running
  to satisfaction rather than left blank.
- **An act**: the act itself and the thing it is performed on.

Where the title states a formula, carry the formula across as the title states it, including its
segmentation. A formula whose base changes partway through the period is two segments, and collapsing
them into one changes the sum.

## 4. The date chain

Dates in this family are derived. Build the chain once, in the notes, and carry a single baseline
through the whole document.

**Step one: name the starting event.** It is a procedural event, not a calendar date someone chose:
the day a decision was served, the day a ruling reached the party, the day a period the decision
itself fixed came to an end. Where the inputs do not record the event but the instruction supplies it,
the instruction's fact is the starting event and is used as given.

**Step two: name the period and where it comes from.** A period is fixed either by the title itself
(the time the decision allowed for performance) or by the procedural rules (the window for bringing
this kind of action, the window for applying for enforcement). Say which, and do not swap one for the
other.

**Step three: state the counting convention you used**, and use it everywhere. The day the starting
event falls on and the day a period expires are conventions, not facts; two defensible conventions
produce dates one day apart, and a filing that uses both is internally inconsistent.

**Step four: derive the downstream dates.** In this family they typically are: the day the period for
performance expired; the day after it, which is when the consequences of not performing begin to run;
the day up to which a running amount is provisionally counted; and the date the filing itself carries.

**Where two baselines are defensible, choose one and say which.** The day a final decision was made
and the day it was served are both arguable starting points, and they give different expiry dates. Pick
the one the inputs support best, state it in the text as the point from which you count, and make
every downstream date follow from that same choice. What fails is not choosing the other one; what
fails is a document in which some dates follow one baseline and some the other.

**Reconcile before you draft.** Check that: the filing's own date falls inside the window that makes it
timely; no date in the filing precedes a document it relies on; a provisional cut-off date is the same
in every item that uses one; and the day a consequence starts running is exactly one day after the day
the corresponding period expired.

When the chain matters to admissibility, as it does whenever a filing has to be made within a window
after an event, the chain is stated in the text, in the opening passage, in as many sentences as it
takes and no more. The reader has to be able to see the filing is in time without doing the arithmetic.

## 5. Which body receives the filing

The forum in this family is fixed by the enforcement procedure. It is not selected by the rules that
would govern a fresh dispute, and the inputs will contain at least one plausible wrong answer.

- **To start enforcement**, the filing goes to the body the enforcement rules designate for the title,
  which is ordinarily the court of first instance in the concluded case or a court of the same level
  where the property is. It is not the body whose decision made the duty final: an appellate decision
  that makes a first-instance judgment enforceable does not make the appellate court the enforcement
  court.
- **For an action that a ruling inside the enforcement case has opened**, the filing goes to the court
  that made that ruling, that is, the court conducting the enforcement. It is not the court that
  decided the underlying dispute, and it is not a court chosen by the defendant's domicile or by any
  agreement in the underlying contract.

State the body once, in the header and again at the sign-off, identically. Do not discuss jurisdiction
in the document; the correct body is named, not argued for, unless the route reference says otherwise.

Verification: the body you named appears in the inputs in the role you are relying on, and you can say
in one sentence which rule put the filing there.
