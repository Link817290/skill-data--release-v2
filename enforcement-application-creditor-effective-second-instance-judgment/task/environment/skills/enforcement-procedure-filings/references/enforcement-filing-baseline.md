# Baseline for every enforcement filing

This reference is the frame that every filing in this family shares, whichever of the two routes the
deliverable takes. The route reference says what goes inside the body; this file says what the
document around it looks like, how the parties are labelled, what it never contains, how a rule is
cited, and how long it runs.

## 1. Read everything before drafting

Open and read every reference in the routing table of the entry point, this one included, before you
write the first line. Then apply only the parts whose conditions match your task. Reading is
unconditional; applying is conditional.

## 2. Designations belong to the enforcement case

The concluded action gave the parties designations that describe what they did in that action. The
enforcement case gives them designations that describe what they are now: the party moving the
enforcement organ, and the party against whom enforcement runs. From the moment the filing is about
enforcement, those are the labels that govern.

**In an application to the enforcement organ**, each party carries its enforcement designation and
nothing else. Do not carry over the designation from the concluded action, and do not add a second
label in brackets. The filing is inside the enforcement case, so no other frame of reference exists
for the reader.

**In a pleading in an action that arises out of the enforcement**, each party carries two labels at
once: its status in this new action, and, immediately after it in brackets, the role it holds in the
enforcement case. This is not decoration and it is not a coined hybrid. The subject matter of the
action is the enforcement itself, so the court has to see from the party block which side of the
enforcement each party sits on; a party block that gives only the action statuses forces the reader
to reconstruct that from the body. Use the enforcement roles the enforcement file itself uses, not
roles you have invented for the pleading.

Three further rules on designations:

- **One designation per party, everywhere.** The header, the body, the closing request and the
  sign-off all use the same label for the same party. A party who is called by its enforcement role
  in one paragraph and by its role in the concluded action in the next has been described twice.
- **Where the inputs label the same party differently in different documents, take the later
  document.** Records of an entity's address and of who represents it go stale; the later of two
  decisions is the later record. Take it, and do not explain in the filing that an earlier document
  said something else.
- **Do not upgrade a role.** Someone the enforcement file records as having raised an objection is
  described as having raised it, not as the owner of the asset or as a buyer in good faith. Those are
  conclusions the filing argues for, not labels it assigns.

## 3. Document frame

- **Title.** The name of the document type, alone on the first line.
- **Party block.** One line per party, in the order the route reference gives. Each line holds the
  party's designation, its name, and, for an entity, the identifiers and the address the inputs give
  and the person who represents it with that person's office; for an individual, the identifying
  particulars the inputs give. Fields come from the inputs; section 6 of
  `unsupported-specifics-and-placeholders.md` governs a field the inputs do not hold.
- **Opening passage.** Short. It fixes the enforcement title, says that it has taken effect, and says
  what has happened since that makes this filing necessary. It never summarises the underlying
  dispute or restates the reasons of the decision.
- **Body.** As the route reference sets out.
- **Closing request or applied-for list.** As the route reference sets out. It is the operative part
  of the document and nothing after it adds to it.
- **Sign-off.** The customary valediction, the name of the body on the next line, then the party's
  name under the same designation as in the header, and the date. Where a schedule is referred to in
  the body, it is named here as an attachment.

## 4. Citing a rule

Enforcement filings rest on a small number of provisions, and most propositions in them are about
procedure rather than about the merits.

- Name the instrument and the article number beside the proposition it decides, and do not quote the
  article's text at length.
- **Never invent an article number.** If you cannot place the provision, state the rule and name the
  instrument only. A wrong number is worse than no number: it is checkable, and it will be checked.
- Where the proposition rests on published guidance rather than on a statute or an interpretation,
  say which body issued the guidance and when, and treat it as persuasive rather than binding. Do not
  stretch guidance past the situation it addressed.
- Cite a provision only where it decides a point. Do not cite one for the act of making the filing.
- The basis of a sum is cited where the sum is, not in a separate part.

## 5. What an enforcement filing never contains

- **Drafting notes.** Remarks about how the inputs were prepared, about missing pages, illegible
  scans, inconsistent numbering, redactions or placeholders, and any statement of what the inputs
  failed to contain. If a fact is missing, the treatment is in
  `unsupported-specifics-and-placeholders.md`; commenting on its absence is never part of it.
- **Concessions against your client.** Calling one of your own items weak, doubtful or possibly out
  of time; conceding that a period may have run; disputing a figure that favours your client;
  offering a reduction nobody asked for.
- **Re-argument of the decided case.** The merits are closed. Recounting how the claim was proved, or
  answering an argument the concluded decision already rejected, belongs to no part of this family.
- **Requests directed at the wrong body.** An application to an enforcement organ does not ask a court
  to decide anything, and a pleading before a court does not instruct the enforcement officer.
- **Procedural applications nobody asked for.** Requests to investigate, to summon, to appraise or to
  order production, including conditional ones, unless the route reference calls for them.
- **Extra parts.** Any section the frame and the route reference do not call for: a chronology, a
  summary of the evidence, a statement of the law separated from the propositions it decides, a
  second conclusions section before the operative part.
- **States of mind the record does not show.** Describe the conduct the documents record and state
  its legal effect; do not convert conduct into knowledge, intent or bad faith.

Counterexample: where the instruction itself asks for something in this list, write it, in its own
part, after the body.

## 6. Consistency

- One position per document. A sentence that helps one item and contradicts another has to go.
- Attribute quoted content to whoever said it. A party's own assertion recorded inside a ruling is
  that party's assertion, not the ruling's finding, and the difference decides cases in this family.
- A later passage may not rely on something an earlier passage rejected.
- Every figure that appears twice appears identically, and any figure derived from others reconciles
  with them.

## 7. Length

Do:

- Follow the operative part's own order and its own numbering.
- Give each item the space its difficulty deserves: an item transcribed from the title is short; an
  item that required a judgement carries that judgement in the text.
- State a point that covers several items once, where those items sit.
- Quote a decision in short phrases only, inside quotation marks.

Do not:

- Restate the operative part twice, once as narrative and once as the applied-for list.
- Reproduce the reasons of the decision that took effect.
- Add tables, indexes or chronologies that repeat the body.
- Pad a part that has nothing in it. A part with no content is removed, not filled.

## 8. Stop conditions

- **Outside the remit.** If the instruction confines you to one filing, do not draft a second one and
  do not write in another party's voice.
- **A point the record cannot decide.** State the narrower proposition the record does support. Do not
  ask, inside the filing, for more material.
- **A part that could be produced by copying.** A part that reproduces columns of the inputs carries
  no position. Either it becomes a position with a reason, or it comes out.
