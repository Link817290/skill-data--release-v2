# Link requirements, events and evidence

## Establish the review boundary

Identify approved project/phase, actual assets under review, monitoring period
and the documents actually supplied. Distinguish the full source dossier from
an extract. A fact found in an author's fuller file is unavailable to the
recipient unless the supplied material includes it. Request that evidence rather
than citing a paragraph that does not contain it.

Crosswalk product names, source IDs, outlet IDs, treatment facilities and units.
Compare both labels and quantities: swapped adjacent identities can preserve
totals. Preserve inconsistent originals and ask for a primary register rather
than moving actual outputs to make a capacity ratio agree. Investment changes
need accounting/scope explanations; a changed amount alone does not establish
a physical or legally material project change.

## Join monitoring events to operating evidence

Use the monitoring event as the unit of review: source/process, location,
date/time, operating mode, pollutant, sampling and averaging period. Link it
to contemporaneous production and treatment operation, not merely to a nearby
calendar date. A factory-wide statement may not describe a particular process.

For example, process A sampled on Tuesday is not covered by process B's Tuesday
log or process A's Monday log. A matching Tuesday record is only a coverage hit:
its hours, mode and relevant equipment status still need inspection. Missing
hour-level information prevents an exact-time match even if the dates agree.

Create three working sets:

- Matched evidence, with its actual scope and relevant parameters.
- Unmatched events requiring a dated record or explanation.
- Conflicting evidence, such as production shown while the relevant treatment
  system was recorded off, requiring reconciliation before the result is used.

Do not infer stability from load percentage alone. Preserve source denominators
and working calendar; the percentage is a derived quantity, not a substitute
for treatment operation. Do not invent minimum operating loads unless the
applicable rule provides them. A missing log is not proof of unstable operation.

## Choose the applicable requirement

Create a criterion card for each comparison:

| Dimension | Needed information |
|---|---|
| Authority | Approval condition, permit, standard or technical method and cited clause |
| Time | Version/effective date, transition requirements and the review event's date |
| Scope | Location, source category/size, existing/new designation and process |
| Measurement | Pollutant, point, unit, reference state and averaging convention |
| Dependencies | Stack/installation conditions, operating mode or special restriction |

Do not blindly choose either the latest standard or the smallest number.
Approvals, permits and generally applicable rules must be reconciled by their
actual scope and transition provisions. A stricter number for another pollutant,
reference condition or source category is not an applicable limit. When priority
or historical applicability cannot be established from the dossier, identify
the unresolved rule and request the authoritative text or determination.

Limits may depend on asset dimensions or site conditions. Distinguish a stated
dimension from an as-built verified dimension and a required condition. Keep a
conditional calculation where useful, but do not adopt a compliance result
based on an unresolved premise. Do not enlarge the phase under review to obtain
more favorable capacities, source coverage or disposal arrangements.
