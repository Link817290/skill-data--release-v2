# Termination settlement execution manual

This manual is for settlements built from many materials, where the evidence conflicts or the
amounts have to be computed. It gives a general method, never an answer to a specific case.
Where the applicable law, region, timing or the materials themselves are insufficient, keep
the conclusion conditional rather than inventing facts.

## 1. Separate four layers of information first

Keep these apart in the working papers; never write them into one cell:

1. **Raw facts from the materials**: document text, table values, signature dates, payment records.
2. **Applicable rules**: regulations attached to the materials, valid agreements, and the professional basis shipped with this skill.
3. **Adoption judgement**: which fact or reading you chose when they conflicted, and why.
4. **Computed results**: amounts produced from the parameters and formulas you already adopted.

A conclusion the material asserts about itself is not automatically a fact. A payroll sheet
labelled "not wages", an employer certificate saying "nothing payable", or minutes recording
"reached by consensus" all still need checking against the applicable rule, who issued it,
when, what it covers, and what was actually performed.

## 2. Intake and timeline

Build a material register first, recording at least: material number, filename, type, issuer,
date created or signed, period covered, whether both parties signed, key subject, readability,
and any duplicate or superseding relationship.

Then run these checks:

- Are the document numbers continuous, and does the count match the number of actual files?
- Any file that will not open, is image-only, is missing a worksheet, has formulas without cached values, or carries hidden content?
- Does the same fact appear in several versions, and does the later one explicitly supersede the earlier?
- Was the document created after the event it attests to — is it a post-hoc one-sided statement?
- Do the approvals, contracts or policies actually cover this person, role, region and the disputed period?
- Are the payroll, attendance, bank or appraisal records continuous month to month, and do the totals reconcile back from the detail?
- Treat "notes", "remarks", "basis names" in tables and employer-produced certificates as claims to be verified, not as the rule itself. Prefer to reconcile from the detail, the payment status and independent materials.
- For government or public data cited in the materials, check the issuer, source page, statistical subject, availability date and intended use as recorded in the shipped files. Distinguish an internal extract, a contribution-system field and a formal official document. Do not visit external pages.

Build a timeline around: hire date, role changes, pay changes, policy effective dates, the
dispute events, the separation discussions, agreement signing, and the final termination.
Resolve date conflicts before computing anything.

## 3. Generate items backwards from the sources of entitlement

Do not wait for the instruction to name the items. Run at least these seven sweeps, adding
candidates to the list each time, even where the final answer will be "not payable":

1. **How the employment ended**: mutual agreement, resignation, contract expiry, unilateral employer termination, unlawful-termination exposure, and any agreed compensation.
2. **Pay during employment**: fixed pay, variable pay, commission, performance pay, bonuses, allowances, subsidies, overtime, arrears, and income already paid that may still belong in a base.
3. **Time and leave**: untaken statutory leave, time off in lieu, overtime records, medical leave periods, and other time entitlements relevant to the settlement.
4. **Post-termination obligations**: non-compete, confidentiality, training service periods, liquidated damages, and continuing payment obligations.
5. **Long-term incentives**: equity, phantom equity, options, co-investment, deferred bonuses, dividends, and vested-but-unpaid entitlements.
6. **Benefits and in-kind arrangements**: housing, vehicle, insurance, supplementary benefits, reimbursements, loans, floats, asset return, and any double-benefit question.
7. **Administrative items**: social insurance, housing fund, individual income tax treatment, certificates to be issued, file transfer, and other closing items the agreement provides for.

Then sweep backwards through three more places: every income category in the pay structure,
every special term in each contract or agreement, and every promise in the termination
documents. Only when all three are done can the candidate list be considered complete.

**Nothing leaves the list silently.** The candidate list is a commitment: every item on it has
to appear in the final schedule with a status, even where that status is "not payable". Items
disappear between the sweep and the delivery for mundane reasons — the analysis got long, the
item looked small, its evidence was awkward — and a missing row is indistinguishable from work
never done. Before you deliver, set the candidate list beside the schedule and check them off
one against the other. An item that was worth adding to the list is worth a row saying what
became of it.

Watch particularly for shortfalls: an amount that was underpaid rather than unpaid. These
surface during the reconciliation rather than in any document that announces them, so they
never arrive with a label, and they are the easiest thing in the packet to lose track of.

## 4. Build a shared parameter ledger

Build the ledger before computing any item. Keep these fields for each parameter:

| Parameter | Raw value | Source and location | Period covered | Adopted value | Reason adopted | Items affected |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Check at least: employment start and end dates, cumulative years of service, the character of
the termination, the trailing twelve-month window, the total for each income item, average
wage, the local cap figures, leave days, amounts already paid, the period still payable, and
the applicable multipliers and percentages.

The same number can serve entirely different regimes. A social-insurance or housing-fund
declaration ceiling, the statistical wage, the severance cap, the minimum wage and the
contract wage must not substitute for one another because their magnitudes are close or
because both are labelled "three times". Recompute every derived figure independently from
the raw annual or monthly value, and check the division, the multiplier and the rounding.

"Average wage" is the worst offender, because several different quantities carry that name in
the same packet: the average of what was actually paid, the average of what was due, the base
declared for tax, the base declared for social insurance, and any average defined by a specific
agreement clause. They are computed over different income sets and often over different months,
so they do not agree. When an entitlement is expressed as a percentage or multiple of "average
wage", go back to the clause that creates the entitlement and read which average it names —
then build that one from the raw monthly figures. A payroll or tax column already labelled
"average" is a convenient number, not necessarily the one the clause points to, and picking it
because it is to hand silently changes every downstream amount that rests on it.

A cell that mixes a raw fact, a judgement and a formula defeats review. Keep the raw value,
the adoption switch and the formula result in separate columns.

## 5. Judging conflicting evidence

### 5.1 Do not apply a mechanical "signed document wins"

Evidential weight depends on what is being proved. Check item by item:

- **Legality**: does it touch a mandatory entitlement that cannot be contracted away?
- **Authority and scope**: was the issuer entitled to make that determination, and does the document cover this employee, role and period?
- **Bilateral character**: a two-party agreement usually outweighs a one-sided statement, but still has to pass legality and genuine intent.
- **When it was created**: a contemporaneous record made before the dispute usually outweighs a post-dispute confirmation.
- **Primacy**: payroll transactions, attendance detail and original approvals usually outweigh a summary certificate.
- **Actual performance**: a long, stable record of what was actually paid or done may change how a contractual label should be read.
- **Specificity**: a record naming the clause, period and amount usually outweighs a general statement.
- **Corroboration**: a fact confirmed by independent sources carries more weight.

Mandatory rules, valid agreements, government approvals, performance records, internal
policies, one-sided certificates and correspondence do not sit in one fixed order that applies
to every question. State why a given piece of evidence is the right one for the fact at hand.

### 5.2 Evidence matrix

For each disputed point, use these fields:

| Fact to prove | Supports | Contradicts | Source location | Effective or covered period | Reliability concerns | Adopted | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Where the evidence cannot settle the point uniquely, output conditional branches and say what
further material would resolve it. Do not write a preference as a settled fact.

## 6. Termination character and the compensation decision

The following is a general checking framework for PRC employment relationships. Where the case
materials or the applicable region provide a different valid rule, record the difference.

### 6.1 Establish the termination path first

In order:

1. Is there a genuine, effective mutual termination agreement?
2. If the employer terminated unilaterally, on exactly what basis, and are both the facts and the procedure satisfied?
3. Is this a statutory expiry or termination that requires severance?
4. Does it amount to unlawful termination, and is the employee claiming continued performance or damages?
5. Is there a valid agreed compensation higher than the statutory standard?

The employer raising the discussion first does not by itself make the termination unlawful; an
effective mutual termination is normally handled at the severance or agreed amount. Conversely,
dressing a unilateral termination up as "consensus" cannot be judged from the document title
alone — check the genuine intent and what was performed.

### 6.2 Judge N, 2N and payment in lieu of notice separately

- The severance month count N is normally the number of complete years of service; a remaining period of six months or more counts as one month, and less than six months as half a month.
- The 2N damages for unlawful termination is a multiple of the statutory severance standard, applied only once unlawful termination is established. It does not follow automatically from the employer having raised the separation first.
- Payment in lieu of notice arises only on the specific statutory unilateral-termination paths and only where thirty days' written notice was not given. A mutual termination does not create or extinguish it merely because the discussion fell more or less than thirty days before the leaving date.
- Where severance, damages, notice pay and an agreed amount all arise from the same facts, state whether they run in parallel, replace one another, or are alternatives. Guard against double counting.

### 6.3 Computing severance

In the ordinary case under Article 47 of the Labour Contract Law:

- `N = complete years + the converted value of the remaining period`.
- `Average wage over the trailing 12 months = total qualifying wages due ÷ 12`; where employment was shorter, use the actual number of months.
- Judge each pay item by its real character. Normally check time-based and piece-based wages, bonuses, allowances, subsidies and other monetary income. Do not rely on the accounting account, the tax category name or the contract label alone.
- For annual bonuses and cross-period performance pay, separate the period in which it was earned from the date it was paid, and decide under the applicable rule whether and how to apportion it.
- Where the high-income cap applies, verify the region, statistical year, issuing body, unit of the figure and the date from which it applies. Check both the capped wage base and the maximum number of compensable years.
- `Severance = applicable monthly wage base × N`. Any 2N multiple is applied separately at the termination-character layer.

If the data that should apply at the termination date had not been published by that date, or
the figures in the materials cannot be verified, compute the amount under each reading and
present them as scenarios. Never manufacture an official figure.

A scenario is not the same thing as a pending item, and the difference decides where the money
sits. A scenario is fully computed — you know the amount under each reading and you say which
reading each rests on; it is published, with its condition attached, outside the confirmed
payable. A pending item has no amount at all. So a figure that depends on a publication date
falling after the termination date is a scenario: work it out, show it, and keep it out of the
confirmed total. Say plainly why the confirmed total excludes it, and state what the total
becomes if the condition turns out to hold.

## 7. Item-specific know-how

### 7.1 Untaken annual leave

Four steps:

1. Determine the statutory annual entitlement from cumulative years of service, not from service at this employer alone.
2. For the leaving year, normally convert as `calendar days elapsed this year ÷ 365 × full-year entitlement − days already taken or scheduled`, and do not pay the fractional part below a whole day.
3. List days carried over from the prior year separately from the converted days for the leaving year, and check whether the carry-over has a factual or policy basis.
4. The daily wage is normally the monthly average over the trailing twelve months excluding overtime pay, divided by 21.75. Where 100% of the wage for normal working time has already been paid, the additional settlement is normally at 200% of the daily wage. Exceptions such as a written waiver by the employee must be judged separately.

Keep every intermediate value in the table: full-year days, calendar-day ratio, days taken or
scheduled, the discarded fraction, carry-over, the daily wage base and the payment multiple.

Clues to cumulative service may sit in the onboarding form, personnel file fields, a former
employer's certificate, social-insurance records or appointment papers. Where the company's
leave system is banded on service at this employer only, go back to cumulative service and
recompute the historical years and the leaving year. Do not merely fix the current year's
fraction.

### 7.2 Non-compete

- First confirm whether the person falls within the scope for which a non-compete may be agreed, along with the restricted business and territory, the start and end dates, and the maximum permitted period.
- Where the agreement sets a compensation standard, judge from that valid agreement. Only where none was agreed and the employee has actually performed do you look to the applicable statutory or judicial-interpretation fallback.
- Distinguish the first month's payable, the monthly standard, the cumulative payable as at the settlement date, and the theoretical total for the whole period. These are four different numbers and they answer four different questions; publish them as separate lines rather than collapsing them into one figure.
- **A continuing monthly obligation does not belong in the one-off settlement total.** The lump sum is what falls due on separation; a payment that recurs monthly for a fixed period afterwards is an ongoing liability and belongs on its own line, outside that total. Adding the whole-period theoretical figure into the lump sum overstates what is payable now by a large multiple; adding only the months elapsed so far quietly makes the total depend on the date the schedule happened to be written. Keep the monthly standard and the term on record, and let the reader multiply.
- Check the minimum-wage floor, release from the restriction where payment is overdue, and any additional payment required where the employer terminates the restriction early.
- Non-compete compensation and termination severance are different in character and must not be offset against each other without a basis.

### 7.3 Working time and overtime

Do not conclude from the job title or a single approval document. Check the approving
authority, the roles covered, the validity period, the employee's actual role, the local rules
for rest days and statutory holidays, what has already been paid, and whether any unpaid claim
remains.

"No further overtime top-up" and "whether historical overtime pay belongs in a particular
average-wage base" are two different questions and must be judged separately.

### 7.4 Performance pay, commission and bonuses

For each variable payment, establish: the period earned, the appraisal condition, the appraisal
result, the due date, the actual payment date, the amount paid, the leaver clause, and the
reason for any incompletion.

Add a horizontal reconciliation table checking, period by period: score band → coefficient the
rule allows → coefficient actually approved → amount due → payroll amount → payment status. At
minimum check:

- Does the coefficient actually applied fall in the band the score maps to? If the approved coefficient sits below the band the appraisal result entitles, the difference is a shortfall that is owed — an underpayment, not a discretionary top-up. It will not be labelled anywhere; it only appears once you line the score against the band against what was actually paid.
- Where the rule gives a band rather than a point, the band **is** the answer. Adopt it as a range: compute the shortfall at the bottom of the band and at the top of it, and publish both as the item's low and high. Do not pick a single coefficient inside the band, and do not push the item aside because you cannot pick one — a stated range is a determination, and a range that propagates into the totals as a low and a high is exactly what the reviewer needs. This item's range also feeds other items' bases, so leaving it out silently changes them too.
- Is "the most recently completed appraisal period" the same as "the most recent period already paid with salary"? Which one the leaver proration cites must follow the agreement text.
- Has an amount that finished approval but has not entered a payroll batch been omitted from the payroll sheet? Conversely, is an amount already in the payroll sheet being treated again as an unpaid balance?
- Where an annual bonus is mixed into a quarterly performance column, has it been separated by earning period, character and payment date?
- An appraisal that is complete but paid late does not lose the entitlement merely because the payment date falls after the leaving date.
- Where the appraisal is not yet complete, check for a valid rule prorating by days in service, by the previous period's coefficient, or by actual completion.
- Never record "not yet determined" as zero, and never turn a habit of past payments into a settled debt.
- The same performance amount must not be counted both into the historical average wage and again as an unpaid balance in the total.

### 7.5 Annual bonus and long-term incentives

- For an annual bonus, check separately the contractual right, the policy conditions, the year earned, any in-service condition, the customary payment timing, and the actual business result.
- For equity, phantom equity, options, co-investment or dividends, first decide whether it is employment remuneration or an investment return, then reconcile vested, unvested, declared-but-unpaid and not-yet-qualified entitlements separately.
- "Unvested and forfeited" and "vested but not yet paid" must sit on separate rows.

### 7.6 Allowances, subsidies and in-kind benefits

Judge by economic substance, not by name:

- A cash allowance paid monthly at a fixed amount and freely disposable by the employee normally has a strong wage character.
- Reimbursement against receipts, a directly provided in-kind arrangement, or a clear expense repayment may sit closer to a benefit or a cost.
- Where a personal contract, a collective rule and an internal policy all exist, check who each applies to, over what period, how conflicts resolve, and what was actually performed. Do not let a general policy label override an explicit individual agreement.
- The bases used for tax reporting, social-insurance contributions, total payroll and a specific statutory entitlement may differ, and cannot substitute for one another.
- Check whether any asset remains held, or any deposit, damage, double benefit or return obligation is outstanding at termination.

## 8. Every result needs an explicit status

Each candidate item carries exactly one of these, with an explanation:

- **Payable**: facts, rule and amount are all determined.
- **Not payable**: there is a clear exclusion basis — not merely that no figure was found.
- **Already settled**: verifiable evidence of both the amount due and the amount paid.
- **Conditionally payable**: the condition and the branch amounts can be stated.
- **Pending evidence**: cannot be determined now; state what material is missing.

A `0` in the amount column means the amount is determined to be zero. A pending item must use a
blank or "pending confirmation"; zero must not be used to paper over uncertainty.

**Pending is the narrowest of the five, not the safest.** It applies where the materials cannot
settle the amount — a parameter that was never published, a period the records do not cover, a
conflict no evidence resolves. It does not apply where you have a complete chain and can also
imagine a further document that would confirm it. Two cases in particular are decisions, not
pending items:

- A figure you derived from a rule in the materials plus parameters you adopted. If the derivation holds, adopt the result. Wanting corroboration from a document that was never part of the packet is not grounds to withhold it.
- A range the rules genuinely allow. Publish the range as the adopted answer, with both ends. A stated range is a determination; moving the whole item to pending because you cannot pick one point inside it discards work you have already done.

Note what parking an item costs. A settlement is read as the set of amounts it actually adopts;
items held back sit outside that set and support nothing, however carefully they were computed.
Where you are confident enough to compute a figure but want the reservation on record, adopt it
and attach the reservation — that keeps both the number and the caveat visible.

## 9. Computation and cross-checking

Run a six-element check on every amount:

1. **Source**: which file, clause or cell the number came from.
2. **Period**: whether it covers the right months or calendar days.
3. **Basis**: gross or net, due or paid, wage or benefit, paid or unpaid.
4. **Unit**: yuan, ten-thousands, month, day, hour and percentages all consistent.
5. **Formula**: inputs, order of operations, where rounding happens, and the multiplier all visible.
6. **Result**: reproduced by hand or by an independent formula.

Then check across:

- Monthly payroll detail sums to the annual or twelve-month total.
- The numerator of the average wage matches the income items listed, over the right number of months.
- The summary total equals the sum of the determined items, and monthly items are not mistakenly counted as a lump sum.
- Amounts already paid are not counted again in the payable total.
- "Due", "entered a batch" and "matched to a bank receipt" are three distinct states in the payroll ledger. Only verifiable proof of actual payment marks an item settled; a future or pending batch stays a balance.
- Excel formulas agree with their cached displayed values, and no key conclusion exists only as a formula result without a stated basis.
- When one disputed parameter is changed, every affected item either moves with it or is explicitly shown to be unaffected.

## 10. Delivery structure

At least four parts:

### 10.1 Parameter table

Fields: parameter, raw value, adopted value, unit, period, source location, reason adopted,
items affected, and whether it is disputed.

### 10.2 Settlement item table

Fields: item, status, amount or disposition, whether it enters the total, base, formula, rule
basis, factual basis, precise location, amount already paid, what evidence is pending, and risk
notes.

### 10.3 Evidence index

Organised by conclusion rather than as a plain list of files: the conclusion, supporting
materials, contradicting materials, the reason adopted, and the precise location.

### 10.4 Open questions

The question, its current impact, the material needed, how the amount changes under each
answer, and who should resolve it. Pending items must not be mixed into a determined total.

## 11. Reverse check before submitting

- Were the candidate sweeps completed across all seven sources of entitlement?
- Does every income category, special term and termination promise have a stated disposition?
- Does every item carry exactly one status, consistent with its amount?
- Did every amount pass the six-element check?
- Does every disputed conclusion show both supporting and contradicting evidence?
- Were a signature, a document title, a tax category or a one-sided certificate mistaken for a conclusive answer?
- Is any amount double counted, any monthly standard treated as a total, or anything already paid paid again?
- Is there any item marked pending while carrying a zero amount?
- Does the output leak any unnecessary name, ID, contact detail, address, account or internal voucher?
- Do the output path, file format, worksheets and key fields meet the task's requirements?
