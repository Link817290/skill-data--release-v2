# Eligibility and revision control

## Separate the gates

A completed physical activity is not necessarily claimable this period. Establish the contractual gates for quantity, quality/acceptance, price and documentary timing. A signed quantity does not establish a price; an approved rate does not establish hours worked. Keep each gate in the register.

Use identifiers at their proper grain. A bill-of-quantities code identifies a priced item; a measurement document identifies evidence; a revision identifies a state of that evidence. Two documents for one item may be additive segments, cumulative snapshots or replacements. Decide which before summing.

## Cumulative quantities

For the same item and measurement basis, current quantity equals the accepted closing cumulative quantity minus the accepted opening cumulative quantity. Keep precision until the contract's stated rounding point. Do not subtract a prior period's current increment from a closing cumulative amount.

A negative signed delta can correct previous measurement. Do not clamp it to zero or reclassify it as an unrelated cash deduction: that can distort work value and percentage-based deductions. If the basis changed, obtain or retain the explicit adjustment bridge instead of forcing a subtraction.

## Replacements and cutoff

Use the controlling replacement record and preserve its link to the superseded record. Merely taking the largest quantity or latest date can be wrong: a later record may be unsigned, outside the cutoff or a different scope. An explicit supersession relationship is stronger than an inferred filename sequence.

When the closing date and document endorsement date differ, use the period rule actually supplied. A later endorsement may belong in the next claim even if the work was physically completed earlier. Carry the accepted cumulative quantity forward so it is not forgotten or double counted next month.

## Hold without losing the item

The pending register should identify the item, missing authority, existing supporting record, required next document and responsible project role. Separate an unapproved proposed price from an approved payable amount. A proposed amount may be retained for follow-up if clearly excluded from the claim; it must not silently enter a subtotal.
