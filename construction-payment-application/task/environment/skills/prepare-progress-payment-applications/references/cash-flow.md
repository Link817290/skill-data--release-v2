# Work value and financing movements

Work value, payment entitlement and transferred cash are separate measures. A cumulative bank total can explain what remains unpaid, but adding that unpaid balance to a new current-period application may double claim an earlier application.

## Engineering advance

Maintain issued, previously recovered, opening balance, current recovery and closing balance. Apply the contractual recovery rate to its defined base, then limit recovery to the outstanding balance when required. The cap is a stock constraint, not another percentage. Do not use the original advance as the remaining balance after prior recoveries.

## Material advance

Maintain a separate ledger by prepaid batch when prices or advance percentages differ. New eligible stored material can create an advance; consumption of a previously prepaid batch can trigger recovery. Recover using the original prepaid basis when the contract requires it, even if replacement stock now has a different purchase price.

The closing material advance is opening balance plus new advance minus recovery. Engineering advance exhaustion does not imply material advance exhaustion. Avoid netting the two stocks merely because they share the word advance.

## Retention and other deductions

Write the retention base explicitly. If the base is earned work, it usually differs from gross cash additions that include a material advance; the project terms control. Signed work credits may reduce the base. Apply any stated cap or release condition separately.

Keep a confirmed utility debit, penalty or other deduction in its proper payment component. Subtracting it once from work value and again from the payment summary double charges the contractor. A blank record is unknown or not supplied, not automatically a confirmed zero.

## Three-column roll-forward

For each category, closing cumulative equals opening cumulative plus current movement. Check additions and deductions separately before netting. A cumulative application may include original advances that are absent from cumulative earned-work value. Label the measures rather than forcing them to reconcile as if they were the same.
