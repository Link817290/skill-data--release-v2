# Assemble the submission

Use the requested form as the receiving team's interface. Preserve required field labels, identifiers and worksheets while improving widths, number formats, grouping and print areas. Use a plain printable hierarchy; colored worksheet tabs are unnecessary.

The summary should show the opening, current and closing movement of each relevant category. Supporting detail should explain current earned-work lines and any financing adjustments without duplicating the same amount under two categories.

A submission letter normally identifies the contract and period, states the current application amount, names the recipient and lists the attachments actually supplied. Check numeric and written amounts if both are used. Phrase the letter as an application for the receiving institution to process, not as an issued approval, payment certificate or bank confirmation.

An attachment index must connect both quantities and prices. List the effective revision and relevant variation/daywork rate authority; a range that silently includes a superseded record can cause the receiving team to assemble the wrong evidence. Mark archived copies separately from operative supporting documents. Use the term advance recovery for amounts returned through a deduction, so the letter does not confuse financing issued with financing recovered.

Only use endorsement statements supplied by the evidence. Leave the project team's signature and seal areas unexecuted. A well-formed draft can be ready for signing without pretending it has been signed.

Make pending matters operational. Identify the missing quantity signature, price confirmation or next-period inclusion, and an appropriate role to follow up. Avoid a generic instruction to check everything. Keep the pending schedule outside the amount being requested unless the contract expressly permits provisional payment.

Render each printed sheet and Word page. Inspect long project labels, narrow money columns, negative amounts, split headers and omitted notes. Numeric validation alone does not catch a workbook that prints as hash marks or a letter that silently loses its attachment list.
