# Payment Attribution And Authority Route

Use this route when a party claims that transfers, worker payments, a site signature, or a witness account changes another party's payment balance.

## Transaction register

Record each asserted payment once with payer, recipient, amount, covered work, date or period, proof, proponent, dispute status, authorization basis, ratification evidence, and proposed accounting effect. Keep alternative totals in separate columns and calculate their difference. A receipt by a worker proves receipt when supported; it does not by itself prove payment to a contractor or an authorized deduction from that contractor's account.

## Authority chain

For each alleged representative act, test five links:

1. identity of the actor;
2. the act actually proved, such as attendance, signature, instruction, receipt, or settlement;
3. authority for that specific act;
4. transaction scope and amount covered by the authority;
5. later confirmation or ratification by the person to be bound.

Do not infer financial authority from a site role, document possession, attendance, or signature alone. State separately what a signature authenticates and what it does not establish.

## Witness boundary and closeout

Separate what a witness personally saw, did, heard, or received from the witness's inference about contracts, agency, or payment attribution. Reconcile only supported, non-overlapping transactions. For every offset or deduction requested in the final submission, verify the underlying payment, its allocation, the authority path, and the absence of double counting. If any link is missing, explain the consequence and request a conditional treatment rather than inventing a fact.
