# Principal-versus-agent evidence matrix

Read this reference whenever another party participates in providing content, access, payment, distribution, fulfillment, or customer support.

## 1. Define the relationship

Write one line for each party pair:

```text
customer | reporting entity | other party | specified good/service | payment flow
```

Do not combine a content-provider relationship with a distribution-channel relationship. An entity may control the customer-facing service while a channel controls only storefront or payment access.

## 2. Test control before transfer

Ask whether the reporting entity obtains any of the following before the customer receives the specified service:

- the good or other asset itself;
- a right to direct another party's service on its behalf; or
- inputs that the entity significantly integrates into a combined output.

For an integration assertion, identify the combined output from the customer's perspective. Hosting several third-party inputs is not automatically significant integration. Look for transformation, interoperability, cross-use, common identity, persistent access, unilateral deployment, or a service that could not be delivered by any one supplier.

## 3. Reconcile indicators with the control conclusion

Use the following matrix. No single row decides the answer.

| Indicator | Evidence that tends toward control | Evidence that tends toward arranging |
| --- | --- | --- |
| primary responsibility | entity promises acceptability, support, continuity, refunds, or resolution | supplier owes performance and resolves failure directly |
| discretion over price | entity sets customer price or key exchange terms | supplier fixes consideration and entity receives a fixed fee |
| inventory or fulfillment exposure | entity bears refunds, chargebacks, replacement, capacity, or post-transfer risk | supplier bears nonperformance and customer remedies |
| content or service direction | entity can approve, remove, alter, combine, or redirect inputs | supplier controls what is delivered and when |
| customer perception | customer looks to entity for the combined service | customer knowingly contracts for a named supplier's service |

Explain conflicting evidence. Do not omit contract language merely because operating practice points the other way. Obtain evidence that the practice is permitted by, or has modified, the enforceable arrangement.

## 4. Read legal labels carefully

Terms such as marketplace, facilitator, vendor, partner, licensee, or reseller do not decide accounting. They can, however, reveal enforceable obligations that contradict the operating narrative. Reconcile:

- who the contract names as customer;
- who owes refunds or dispute resolution;
- who can remove content or terminate access;
- whether licenses are perpetual, revocable, exclusive, or limited;
- who sets the price or exchange mechanism; and
- whether another party ever controls the specified service before the end user.

## 5. Design audit work

Select contracts and transactions from each material party relationship. Inspect both standard terms and exceptions. Corroborate contract terms with system and operating evidence:

- support tickets and dispute outcomes;
- refund and chargeback records;
- pricing tables and approval logs;
- content approval, removal, and update logs;
- channel service-level agreements;
- supplier settlement reports; and
- customer-facing receipts and terms.

Reconcile gross billings to revenue, amounts retained by other parties, and expense classifications. A conclusion about control does not by itself prove completeness or accuracy of the recorded amounts.
