# Scenarios and Forecast Reconciliation

## Build a forecast that can be challenged

Start with an adopted actual, a comparable growth series and the remaining forecasting period. State the driver behind the projected change: acquisition capacity, retention, product demand, partner distribution, seasonality or another supported mechanism. Distinguish evidence for the driver from an assumption about its future magnitude.

Annualising an interim run rate provides a reference point rather than an automatic forecast. Derive the implied remaining-period amount as the annual forecast minus the disclosed year-to-date amount. Compare it with prior comparable quarters, current production capacity and seasonal concentration. If a strong opening period accounts for much of the half-year total, explain why the second half should or should not repeat that performance.

Use a cautious, central and stronger case only when their different assumptions are meaningful. Label an unsupported baseline as unavailable. A scenario estimate may fill a modelling need when clearly identified as such, but cannot become a purported disclosed historical actual. Do not infer a channel's prior sales solely from the desired growth rate.

## Reconcile components and totals

For point forecasts, add every included channel once and compare the sum with the stated company total. If the reporting taxonomy includes a residual or unmapped population, identify it rather than counting overlapping channels twice.

For intervals, the sum of lower endpoints and sum of upper endpoints define a feasible aggregate envelope. The company interval must overlap that envelope under consistent assumptions. Endpoints need not coincide: individual low cases may not happen simultaneously. Explain material dependence between channels when a single market or distribution assumption affects several components.

Calculate company growth against the prior year's total on the adopted forecast basis. Do not use accounting revenue as the denominator of a signed-premium forecast. A revised channel assumption must flow through the total, the implied growth, the executive conclusion and any chart.

As a synthetic check, intervals of 8 to 11 and 3 to 5 permit a combined range of 11 to 16. A central total of 14 is feasible; a total of 19 is not, unless another non-overlapping component is explicitly present. This feasibility check does not determine which scenario is commercially credible.

## Separate arithmetic from judgement

A mathematically reconciled forecast may still require implausible productivity or an unsupported recovery. Identify the assumption most likely to fail and the evidence that would cause a revision. Where uncertainty cannot be quantified, qualify the conclusion rather than inventing a probability. Source citations verify inputs; they do not turn an expert assumption into a disclosed fact.
