# Measurement and Period Controls

## Select a comparable pair

Create a key for each observation: entity, metric, recognition basis, coverage dates, population, unit and actual/forecast status. Compare observations directly only when the relevant keys agree. Signed premiums, accounting insurance revenue and cash collections can have different recognition timing and scope. A numerical difference between them is not a growth calculation.

When annual and interim channel taxonomies differ, build a mapping with three states: directly comparable, comparable only after an evidenced adjustment, and unresolved. A residual channel may include several populations. Do not assume that its growth measures the growth of a narrower predecessor category. Explain approximate comparisons as context rather than reporting them as disclosed same-basis growth.

If a report changes accounting standards, inspect the notes field by field. A change affecting profit and the balance sheet need not affect a separately disclosed premium metric. Use restated comparatives when supplied. Without restatement, show the levels with the basis identified and avoid a false same-basis growth conclusion. Do not manufacture an adjustment from the difference in totals.

## Resolve quarter and cumulative columns

Record the dates behind every column before using its number. Where successive cumulative periods have the same scope, derive the new quarter as the later cumulative amount minus the earlier cumulative amount. Validate the result against any independently disclosed current-quarter amount. If they disagree beyond rounding, investigate scope changes before forecasting.

Compute quarter-on-quarter growth from the current quarter divided by the preceding quarter, less one. Compute annual growth from comparable full-year observations. An interim cumulative growth rate is not a current-quarter growth rate, and a share of last year's full-year total is not year-on-year growth.

For a disclosed change in a ratio, subtract the prior ratio from the current ratio to obtain percentage points. Divide only when a relative percentage change is actually intended. Retain the labels so these two results cannot be confused.

## Preserve the measured population

A company's renewal premium can exceed the premium of an individual channel. That does not make it a valid channel renewal base. Attribute it to the company unless a source supplies the allocation. The same rule applies to new-business value, policy persistence, staff counts and product sales: identify their covered population before using them to explain a channel.

As a synthetic check, suppose cumulative sales move from 120 to 205 while a current-quarter column reports 85. The quarterly bridge reconciles. If the later amount covers an additional business line, subtraction alone does not establish comparable organic growth. A change in population must change the comparison decision, even when the arithmetic still works.
