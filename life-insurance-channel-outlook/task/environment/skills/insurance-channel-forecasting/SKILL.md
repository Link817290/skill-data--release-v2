# Insurance Channel Forecasting

Use this method when an insurer's annual disclosures and interim solvency reports must support a business outlook and channel forecast. It applies to overlapping reporting periods, changing accounting bases and channel definitions, rather than assuming every published premium figure belongs to one time series.

## Establish the evidence model

Before calculating growth, identify the reporting entity, metric, accounting basis, period, channel population, unit and source page for each adopted input. Preserve a distinction between a disclosed actual, a management projection and your own assumption. A matching label is insufficient when the population or measurement basis differs. Consult [references/measurement-and-periods.md](references/measurement-and-periods.md) whenever two candidate inputs differ on these keys or an interim report contains both current-quarter and cumulative columns.

Keep source figures at their original precision for arithmetic. Round only display values. A comparison is complete when both inputs and the resulting relationship can be reconstructed from the report's cited evidence. Do not treat an input copied into a table as an analytical conclusion.

## Form the business assessment

Separate scale, capital capacity, value creation and distribution capability. Explain how the evidence supports or limits the outlook. Capital ratios describe capacity and resilience; they do not by themselves establish future sales. Product mix, renewal persistence, acquisition activity and agent productivity can point in different directions. Explain the disagreement instead of turning every positive indicator into an independent growth driver.

For risks, link the observed exposure to a mechanism, the affected forecast assumption and a monitoring or mitigation response. Distinguish company-wide exposures from channel-specific evidence. A regulatory event's publication date and the period of the underlying conduct answer different questions. Do not invent an industry benchmark to support a relative ranking.

## Build and reconcile the forecast

For each channel, use comparable disclosed periods to establish the run rate, then state the assumption that changes the remaining period. Test whether the implied remaining-period performance is credible. Use [references/scenarios-and-reconciliation.md](references/scenarios-and-reconciliation.md) for seasonality, alternative scenarios, range aggregation and cases where the source does not disclose a usable baseline.

Keep the channel forecasts and the company total on one adopted measurement basis. Propagate revised assumptions through tables, summaries and charts. Clearly separate a scenario outcome from a disclosed fact. A projection can differ from a prior expert estimate when its inputs, conditions and arithmetic are defensible.

## Close the report

Recompute growth and shares from the final adopted inputs. Check that channel totals reconcile, quarterly periods bridge to cumulative periods, and forecast totals remain feasible. Verify that numerical chart labels represent the same adopted figures as the text. Check the exported report itself for readable labels, units and visible footnotes; a correct working calculation does not guarantee a correct PDF export.

Stop a quantitative comparison when its population or accounting basis cannot be established. Explain the limitation and continue with supported directional evidence. Do not replace missing disclosure with a plausible-looking actual, attribute company-wide figures to a channel, or imply a causal explanation that the supplied evidence cannot distinguish from an assumption.
