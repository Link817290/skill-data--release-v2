# Closure and Quality Review

## A conclusion needs a downstream action

For an established arrangement, identify the records to preserve and the process that can close. If an arrangement lacks sufficient evidence or remains unresolved, specify the missing evidence, its owner and the decision that must remain open. An investigation request is more useful when it names the underlying fact to establish, rather than merely saying "please verify".

Keep a payroll review distinct from a finding that money is already owed. Record the current payroll state, the unresolved factual prerequisites and the next decision owner. Where statutory sources, salary bases or controlling dates are missing, state the limitation rather than inventing them.

## Calculations that can be replayed

Keep source date and timestamps alongside the elapsed interval. Use a formula or an explicit arithmetic explanation; convert minutes to decimal hours consistently. Check overnight visits, repeated swipes, missing exits and dates before interpreting a subtraction. Preserve the distinction between an interval, a registered leave duration and a payable duration.

## Follow-up controls

Each open item should state the affected event, disputed fact, specific evidence to obtain, responsible roles, review milestone and downstream decision. Separate the evidence collector from the decision approver where those roles differ. Use the applicable policy or business cycle for timing; do not make up an exact service deadline.

## Final reconciliation

Compare the final event set with the source event index. Check that the same person, period and event are used in every related record. Review all event summaries and detailed rows for conflicting adopted conclusions. Confirm citations point to actual relevant records, not merely to files with similar names. Read the report against the workbook and preserve unresolved status in both.

Corrections should preserve the underlying source history. Recommend a targeted control that addresses the observed record failure, identifies an owner and states where in the workflow it belongs. Avoid broad policy advice unrelated to the findings.
