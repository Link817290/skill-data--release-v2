# Claim-to-evidence review

Make a compact list of consequential claims: the adopted alternative, feasibility, objective quality, a trade-off, and an implementation prerequisite. For each, identify the governing source, the calculation or observation that supports it, and the scope in which it holds.

Use the actual delivered report and workbook. A claim present in working notes but omitted from the report cannot help its reader; a figure in the report that differs from the saved workbook needs reconciliation. Inspect currency, population, scenario and period together before comparing two values.

Challenge a strong word with a concrete counterexample. Can another feasible allocation improve the declared first objective? Does a recommended plan lose on a lower-priority metric? Would a valid demand outside a travel limit disappear from the denominator? Resolve the assumption using the supplied business rule, not the convenience of the current result.

Distinguish complete checks from missing evidence. If a path cannot be validated, do not describe its time as verified. If a solver stops without a bound, preserve the feasible incumbent and its limitation. Retain independently established findings while identifying the precise unsupported portion.

Finally inspect whether the report identifies what can proceed now, what remains conditional, and which actual record or authorization would resolve the condition. Later passing checks or polished formatting do not establish operational readiness.
