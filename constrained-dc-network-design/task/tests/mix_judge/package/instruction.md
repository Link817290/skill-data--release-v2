# Compact distribution-center footprint and outage-recovery decision

Operations leadership must set the smallest current-day distribution-center footprint that preserves the maximum service the supplied network can support. Among footprints with that same service and facility count, variable cost determines the recommendation. The demand is in `Supply chain logisitcs problem.xlsx`; `OrderList` carries the day's demand and historical route fields, while the other sheets carry the current network constraints.

Leadership also needs the operating boundary if any facility used by the recommended footprint becomes unavailable. Evaluate each such single-facility outage while allowing any other current facility to receive recovery flow; for every outage, report maximum recoverable service, minimum variable cost, and the split between orders with no remaining current path and orders with a remaining path but no capacity. Do not describe lost eligibility as a capacity requirement. Identify the governing outage as the one with the lowest recoverable service (using higher minimum variable cost to break a service tie). Include a complete order-level recovery allocation and facility reconciliation for that governing outage. Do not invent capacity, routes, rates, or service for unsupported orders.

Build an English-language planning workbook at `/workspace/network_design_recommendation.xlsx`. It must show the recommended footprint, the primary allocation, every primary exception, the full outage comparison, the governing recovery allocation, and decision-ready cost, capacity, service, and exception implications. Company-arranged transportation is limited to current `AIR` rates; `CRF` remains customer-arranged with no company freight cost. Keep the source workbook read-only, use only materials under `/workspace`, do not use the internet or rely on information not supplied there, and retain formulas with cross-sheet lineage sufficient to audit individual decisions, scenario comparisons, and summaries.
