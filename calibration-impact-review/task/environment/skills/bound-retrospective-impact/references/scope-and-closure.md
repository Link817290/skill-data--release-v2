# Scope and closure

For each adopted claim record: system, measurement period, failure criterion, supplied linking evidence, affected record set and confidence. A timestamp alone is not necessarily a unique event key. Distinguish control artifacts from customer workload and process qualification from product specifications.

Use three outcomes when evidence warrants them: a confirmed action, a conditional action awaiting a named record, or no supported extension beyond the observed check. A missing history is not a zero count. Differences between two reported populations may reflect scope or a missing attachment; they do not identify missing individual records.

An actionable follow-up names who should retrieve which evidence and what closes the issue. Examples include approved repeat results, an authoritative work-order association or a formally authorized change to the process baseline. Later passing checks do not by themselves close earlier failures. Do not attribute cause from a pattern alone.

Keep an audit distinction between retaining a recorded pointwise pass and approving the entire process for a new use. Reports should make that distinction understandable without requiring the reader to know statistical terminology.

Completeness is separate evidence: a single valid link proves that item is associated, not that every associated item is listed. Supply `documented_complete_groups` only for groups whose full workload coverage is explicitly established by an authoritative register or closure evidence. Omitted coverage leaves the group unresolved even when known items exist; an invalid item in a declared complete group also leaves it unresolved. The helper cannot verify the truth of the completeness declaration.
