# Organising a quotation into sections

Use this when deciding how to arrange the lines of a quotation.

## The principle

Sections exist so that the customer can accept, remove or renegotiate one part of the job without touching the others, and so that each crew can see its own scope in one place. Group lines by where they are installed and by who installs them, not by what they are made of or by the order the surveyor wrote them.

## A common arrangement for installation jobs

| Section | What belongs there |
|---|---|
| Equipment at the points of use | devices installed where the service is delivered |
| Central equipment | equipment installed at, or administered from, a central room, cabinet or office |
| Installation works and materials | cabling, containment, civil and building works, bases and fixings, measured on site |

Adapt the arrangement to the job. A subsystem large enough to be negotiated on its own can be its own section; keep its fixings with it.

## Deciding doubtful lines

* Works that another trade builds, or that must be finished before equipment goes in, belong with installation works even when equipment later stands on them.
* Items the customer supplies stay visible in their section, with a note, so the scope boundary is clear.
* Percentage charges and tax are not sections; they sit below the sections.
