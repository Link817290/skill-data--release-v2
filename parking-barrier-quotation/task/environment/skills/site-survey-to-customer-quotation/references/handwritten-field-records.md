# Reading handwritten field records

Use this when the survey record is a photograph of handwriting.

## Where entries hide

* **Between lines.** A late addition is often squeezed above or below the line it relates to, in smaller writing. It frequently carries its own figure.
* **In the margin beside a line amount.** A second figure beside a line may be an add on rather than a correction.
* **Under a correction.** Digits written over other digits record a change made on site. Take the final written value and note the correction.
* **Under a closing rule.** A rule under a group of lines often introduces a group total; decide whether the next figure is a total or a new entry.

## Unit price or line amount

A figure beside a quantity can be either. Decide by closure: compute the record's total both ways and keep the reading that reproduces the stated total. Without a stated total, prefer the reading consistent with similar entries.

## Reconciliation routine

1. List every entry with its final figure.
2. Sum them.
3. A shortfall against the stated total usually equals an entry you have not found; search the photograph for a figure of that size.
4. An excess usually means something was counted twice.

## Unclear item names

Before reading doubtful words, write down the kinds of items this type of job needs, using the legible entries and the photographs as your guide. Compare the letter shapes of each doubtful word against that list. Do not let the figure beside a word decide what the word is: different items often share a price. Check any count you read against what the photographs show.
