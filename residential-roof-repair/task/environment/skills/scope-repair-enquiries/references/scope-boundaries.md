# Selecting a repair scope from a wider bill

Build a working crosswalk with source identifier, building or zone, location, work stage, unit, quantity and scope decision. Use the supplied descriptions rather than item-code uniqueness: the same code can recur across buildings.

There are three useful decisions: wholly included; wholly outside; shared or unresolved. A demolition line naming two locations is one combined quantity unless a breakdown is supplied. Do not subtract a replacement quantity to invent its demolition breakdown. New waterproofing may include upstands while stripped area uses another basis.

Waste haulage, access equipment and protection often serve several trades. The presence of roof work in the same bill does not establish that the entire transport volume belongs to roofs. Retain the item, explain the requested boundary and identify what measurement or breakdown is needed. Allocation by floor area is a hypothesis until supported by the source or an agreed measurement rule.

Example: a refurbishment has roof stripping, facade removal and one waste item. Selecting roof stripping does not select the whole waste volume. Conversely, omitting transport because its volume is unknown would leave an execution gap. Carry a pending roof-waste item while retaining any explicit haul distance.

Original whole-project conditions do not automatically bind a newly commissioned subset. Carry applicable technical specifications; distinguish full-contract dates and commercial arrangements from subsection terms that have not been agreed. Do not represent a derived enquiry as an amendment already approved by the original procuring organization.
