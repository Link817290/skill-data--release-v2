# Keep an enquiry usable without inventing commitments

Check what the next recipient actually needs: a consistent scope, quantities or a visible pending basis, technical obligations, and a place to quote. No supplied contractor prices means price fields stay available for quotation; a blank amount is not a zero-price offer.

For a pending quantity, retain the work item and its unit. State the unresolved boundary, what evidence would settle it and who must coordinate confirmation using roles present in the materials. Where those roles are unspecified, describe the confirmation needed rather than inventing an approval chain or deadline. The enquiry can proceed with explicit exclusions or provisional bases, but an unconfirmed quantity is not ready for final settlement.

Track each shared item as one unresolved scope question across the bill, scope note and confirmation list. The physical work may be included while its attributable volume remains unknown; those are compatible statements. Distinguish evidence that the work exists, evidence assigning its quantity to this scope, and evidence that the quantity has been confirmed. A later record supporting only one of these does not resolve the others. Update the same item consistently across documents instead of creating separate assumed quantities for each document.

Measures and related fees must have a clear home: included in composite rates or separately identified. Do not treat every generic measure as physically necessary regardless of site conditions. Ask for the contractor's applicable scheme and price basis. Cross-check overlaps such as transport versus secondary handling, access equipment versus scaffolding, and built-in preparation versus separate layers.

Separate planned, checked, quoted, approved and completed status. An existing bill proves listed scope and planned quantities, not a recent measurement, completed repair or accepted account. Recommended pre-work verification should remain a future action.

For an independent review, try three readings: (1) reconcile facts to source records; (2) propose an alternative scope interpretation and test whether evidence rules it out; (3) simulate a contractor preparing a price and a property manager approving the scope. Any ambiguity that changes work or cost needs resolution or an explicit pending condition.
