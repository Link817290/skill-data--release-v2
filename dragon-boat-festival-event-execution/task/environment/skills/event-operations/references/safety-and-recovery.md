# Safety and Recovery

Use this branch only for risks connected to the stated activities, equipment, site or participants. Avoid importing a different project's hazards.

For each material hazard, connect a prevention measure to an owner and a trigger. Exposure to weather calls for a conditional decision and communication path; lack of actual forecast data is not evidence of a particular weather event. Tools and vulnerable participants call for suitable supervision and a safe interruption path. Medical emergencies require a qualified responder and escalation, not improvised clinical treatment instructions.

A missing essential performer or instructor is a dependency failure. Decide who confirms the delay, how participants wait safely, who may authorize a substitute or pause, and how the changed status reaches other posts. A fallback asset can be proposed but cannot be described as already available without evidence.

Distinguish controlled audience interaction from unrestricted access to work areas. A blanket ban that cancels an approved supervised interaction is not a sound safety plan; permission and oversight should make the authorized operation safe.

Close recovery by checking restored equipment, material balances, reported damage, unresolved issues and the next operating period's dependencies. Media handoff needs selection, a responsible recipient and any applicable approval before external publication. Do not invent a channel, signatory or approval already obtained.
