# Evidence and Preservation

Use this procedure to keep a focused technical correction from turning into an
unsupported rewrite.

## Bind edits to necessity

For each substantive edit, record the baseline location, proposed result, and
the defect or downstream dependency that requires it. If no such dependency
exists, preserve the original content. Formatting, grammar, numbering, and
strictly equivalent notation may change locally, but they must not alter a
scientific relation or create avoidable review work.

Review protected content at the level at which damage occurs: variable meaning,
association rule, objective, constraint, proof step, algorithm transition,
figure, value, author block, or citation. A broad statement that “the model is
unchanged” does not excuse a contradictory local edit.

## Respect the evidence boundary

Tie every empirical claim to a configuration, data or code source, baseline,
metric, randomness policy, and figure or table. When raw assets are unavailable:

- retain accurately described archived results;
- do not regenerate or adjust numerical values by guesswork;
- do not relabel old figures as validation of a changed implementation;
- weaken affected claims or state the need for future validation.

Metadata follows the same rule. Preserve authorship, affiliations, funding,
citations, and submission details unless confirmed records require a change.

## Final preservation audit

After a clean build, compare the draft and revision by semantic unit, not just by
page count or token difference. Inspect every changed block and its neighboring
dependencies. Check equations, algorithms, figures, captions, values, references,
and cross-references in the PDF. Separate harmless layout movement from semantic
change and record every unresolved discrepancy.
