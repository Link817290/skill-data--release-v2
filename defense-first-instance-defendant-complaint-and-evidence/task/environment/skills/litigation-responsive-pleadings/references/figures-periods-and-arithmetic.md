# Figures, periods and arithmetic

Read this before writing any sentence in which a ground turns on an amount, a rate, a base, a period, a
due date or the order of events. A figure that arrives without its derivation, and a period that arrives
without its endpoint, are the two most common half answers in a responsive pleading: the tribunal can
see what you conclude but cannot check it, so it is not a holding it can adopt.

## 1. Build a figure ledger before you argue about any number

**Operation.** One row per number the other side relies on: the number, the document and place it comes
from, what it is composed of, and what the other side uses it for. Add a row for every number you intend
to use yourself, with the same columns.

Then mark each row: taken from the record as it stands; derived by you from figures in the record; or
neither. Rows in the third category cannot be used until they move into one of the first two. A
benchmark, a market rate or an official index that is not in the file is in the third category even
though it is real in the world.

**Verification.** No number appears in the draft that is not in a row, and no row is in the third
category.

## 2. Recompute, do not accept

**Trigger.** Any figure the other side asserts, and any figure carried over from a decision or an
appraisal.

**Operation.** Re-derive it from its components. Where the components are in the file, the derivation
belongs in your text next to the conclusion: base, adjustments, result. Where your figure and theirs
differ, take the difference and identify what it is. A difference that resolves exactly into one
identifiable item is the strongest form of this argument, because it names the error instead of alleging
one.

**Verification.** For every figure you dispute you can state, in one sentence, what the correct figure is
and what the gap consists of. "Their figure is unsupported" without the gap is the weaker version.

## 3. A composite base with an item that does not belong

**Trigger.** A penalty, an interest claim or a damages claim is computed on a base that aggregates
several items, and you say one of those items is not payable.

**Operation.** Say which item, give its own amount, and show the subtraction. Where the item's amount is
not stated separately anywhere, derive it from the quantities and the unit figure the record does give,
and show that derivation too. Then state the consequence: the base is wrong, and the claim computed on
it is wrong to that extent.

**Verification.** The passage contains the composite figure, the excluded item's figure, the arithmetic
linking them, and the reason the item is excluded. Any of the four missing leaves the tribunal unable to
adopt the point.

## 4. Periods

**Trigger.** Any argument about time: a limitation or preclusion period, a payment term, a warranty
period, a notice period, the time for taking a step.

**Operation.** Every period argument has four parts and needs all of them in one place:

1. the start event, and where the record fixes it;
2. the length, and where it comes from;
3. the computed endpoint, stated as a date;
4. the consequence of the comparison - the endpoint against the date of the act in question.

Where the start event is disputed, run the computation on each candidate start and say that the ground
fails on either.

**Verification.** A date appears as the endpoint, and the sentence after it compares that date with
something. A conclusion that a period has run, without the endpoint, is a half answer.

**Counterexample.** Do not start the clock from an event the record does not fix. If the start is
unknown, that itself is the answer: a period that has not begun cannot have expired. Note that this
answer and an unconditional expiry argument are mutually exclusive; see the entry's step 5.

## 5. Interest and contractual penalties

**Trigger.** A claim for interest, liquidated damages or a penalty.

**Operation.** Four elements, each attackable separately and each needing its own basis: the base, the
rate, the start date, and the end date. Work through them in that order and say which of them you
dispute and why.

- **Base**: see section 3.
- **Rate**: where the instrument states a periodic rate, converting it to an annual equivalent is
  arithmetic on a figure that is in the record, and is legitimate. Comparing it with an external
  benchmark is not, unless the benchmark figure is in the file; argue the comparison qualitatively, ask
  the tribunal to apply the standard, or attribute the figure to whoever supplied it.
- **Start date**: it runs from when the sum became due, which means you must first establish when that
  was. Where the due date depends on an event that never happened, the interest claim fails with the
  principal. Where the instrument names a date that is inconsistent with its own payment term, compute
  the term's date and set the two against each other.
- **End date and reduction**: where the amount is out of proportion to the loss actually suffered, that
  is a separate request with its own standard, and it must be made as a request, naming the measure you
  ask the tribunal to apply. Asking for a reduction without naming the measure, or naming the measure
  without asking, is half the move.

**Verification.** For each of the four elements, your draft either disputes it with a reason or is
silent by choice. No element is disputed by assertion alone.

## 6. Payment ledgers and who paid whom

**Trigger.** The parties disagree about how much has been paid, or about whether a payment counts.

**Operation.** Tabulate: date, amount, payer, payee, instrument or record, and what the other side says
about it. Then deal separately with the two questions that get conflated: whether the payment was made,
and whether it discharges the obligation. A payment to someone other than the obligee discharges the
debt only if that person was entitled to receive it, so the authority question needs its own evidence
and its own date range - a payment inside an admitted period of authority stands on different ground from
one outside it.

**Verification.** Every disputed payment has both answers: it happened, and it counted. Totals in your
text reconcile with the ledger.

**Counterexample.** Do not assert payments the ledger cannot source. Where the file records some
payments and suggests others, claim the recorded ones and say that the remainder is to be determined
from the banking records; see the entry's step 4.

## 7. Cross checking a chronology

**Trigger.** You state a sequence of events, or you infer something from an order of events.

**Operation.** Put every date you rely on in one list with its source. Check that the list is consistent
with every other date your draft asserts, including dates inside quotations and dates implied by phrases
such as before, after, during and by then. Where you infer a motive or an intention from the order of
events, state the two events, their dates, the gap, and the inference, and keep the inference labelled
as one.

**Verification.** No date in the draft contradicts another. Every inference from sequence names the
events it rests on.

## 8. Quantities, units and pricing mechanisms

**Trigger.** The dispute is about how a price was to be computed, and different instruments imply
different mechanisms.

**Operation.** A measurement is evidence of the mechanism that uses it. Where the parties recorded a
quantity in the unit that only one candidate mechanism needs, that record is an argument about which
mechanism they were operating, not merely a number. Say which unit, which mechanism it belongs to, and
why the other mechanism would have had no use for it.

**Verification.** The passage connects the unit to the mechanism to the conclusion about which
instrument governs. A bare recitation of the measured quantity does not.

## Stop conditions

- If a figure you need is not in the record and cannot be derived from the record, do not supply it.
  Say what is missing, say who bears the burden on it, and where a procedure exists to determine it, ask
  for that procedure.
- If two figures in the record conflict and nothing resolves them, present both with their sources and
  say which one you contend for and why. Do not silently pick one.
