# Handover and follow-up

Separate a planned service-restoration milestone from permission to restore.
Tests, reassembly, site condition and the responsible operator's authorization
must be satisfied in actual execution. A planning workbook must not fabricate
these outcomes or imply that scheduling itself authorizes switching.

For each follow-up identify triggering event, duration or due rule, coverage,
responsible capacity and evidence needed to close it. Continuous standby can span
nonworking days; response time and monitoring period are different obligations.
When a dedicated support team covers multiple completed areas, verify that the
public plan states this rather than assuming free additional labor.

Later condition checks and reports do not disappear when site work ends. Carry
them into the handoff with provisional dates tied to actual start events. If a
material delay changes the work, propagate it through coupled tests, restoration,
support and later checks.

Work-package boundaries may differ from physical isolation and restoration boundaries.
When circuit-to-asset associations are missing, identify that gap explicitly; do
not invent a precedence map or infer independent energization from package dates.
Use provisional handback milestones and update downstream obligations once the
responsible operator confirms the actual restoration grouping.
