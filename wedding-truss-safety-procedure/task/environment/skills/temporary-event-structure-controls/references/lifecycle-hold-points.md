# Lifecycle Hold Points

Use this reference when several source documents describe the same event from different roles or phases. Extract dependencies rather than copying their heading order. General venue decoration schedules may need a more specific structural or electrical hold point before decorative work can proceed.

For each phase, connect a physical prerequisite to the operation and a release check. Separate venue permission from structural readiness, equipment energization and final use. Put responsibility with the appropriate function and identify who receives a problem when it cannot be corrected by the crew.

Track load-bearing supports through installation and removal. The support that makes a frame stable must remain until the dependent elements are safely removed or another designed restraint takes over. Test the reverse sequence explicitly. Electrical isolation, removal of suspended components and access control must be placed before the actions that would expose workers or guests to their hazards.

Turn risk descriptions into inspections: name what is observed, the unacceptable state, the corrective action and the point where work resumes. Joint venue and project acceptance is distinct from a crew's own check. Leave sign-off entries uncompleted until an actual inspection is available.

Keep fire access, protective surfaces and cable crossings live throughout the lifecycle rather than checking them once at the end. Rehearsal and ceremony staffing should respond to named failure types and carry a handover route. After teardown, reconcile equipment accountability with surface restoration and venue release.

Verification: walk through the document as a crew arriving without its author. If the order could authorize loading an incomplete frame, energizing exposed work, concealing an unresolved hazard or removing stability early, revise the dependency before polishing the layout. Do not invent an actual event timeline when none has been supplied.
