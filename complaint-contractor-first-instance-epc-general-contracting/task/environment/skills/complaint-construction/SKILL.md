# Drafting a construction contract complaint for the contractor side

This skill covers complaints by the party that built, or was engaged to design and build, against the
party that ordered the works. The disputes it handles are over the price and its settlement basis,
variations and visas, quantity changes, delay and its attribution, quality objections, the employer's
termination, and the priority right that secures the price.

## Before you draft: read every reference

Before writing anything, open and read every reference listed in the routing table at the end of this
file, all of them, in full, whatever the case looks like. Then apply each part whose condition matches
your case: a section whose trigger is absent is read and set aside, not applied.

## What goes to the court, and what stays in your notes

The reader is the filing clerk and then the judge. The clerk checks that the parties, the addressee
court, and each request are definite. The judge reads each request and looks, in the facts and reasons,
for the facts and the rule that make it grantable. Everything in the complaint serves one of those two
reads, and nothing else goes into it.

The complaint contains the caption, the parties, the requests, the facts and reasons, the closing, and
the annex list, in that order; `references/document-form-and-length.md` gives the skeleton, what each
part may and may not contain, and the full list of what never appears in the filing.

Your working notes are separate and never delivered. They hold the source table, the claim map, the
forum analysis, the figures you rejected and why, the grievances you decided not to sue on and why, the
attribution and computation tables, and every gap you found in the file. None of this is copied into the
complaint, not as a closing note, a remark after the date, a bracketed aside, or a section of its own.

Five things above all never appear in the complaint: drafting notes of any kind; a forum or
jurisdiction section, an arbitration discussion, or a note beside the court's name; a concession against
the plaintiff; a procedural application the client did not ask for; and a rebuttal of a defence the
defendant has not raised. A short answer to a defence the inputs show the defendant has already put
forward belongs in the facts and reasons, next to the request it threatens.

## Workflow

1. **Sort the sources and build the claim map** as `references/drafting-method.md` sections 1 and 2
   describe. For a construction file, the typical rows are: unpaid price; interest on it; added work;
   adjustment of a unit price after a quantity cut; costs caused by the employer's failures; material or
   labour price adjustment; losses after a wrongful termination and termination itself; the priority
   right; liability of a head company or a sole shareholder.
2. **Fix the price** with `references/construction-price-and-priority.md`: settlement basis and the
   defences already raised against it, the payable date, interest, quality objections, the priority
   right.
3. **Work the changes and the time line** with `references/construction-changes-and-delay.md`:
   variations and their valuation layer, disputed visas, unit price adjustment with its direction, the
   planned start and contractual completion, attribution in your notes, employer failures, price
   changes.
4. **If the employer terminated or took work away**, and in every case for the caption and the court,
   use `references/construction-termination-parties-forum.md`.
5. **Write the requests, then the facts and reasons**, as `references/drafting-method.md` sections 3
   and 4 describe, in the skeleton of `references/document-form-and-length.md`.
6. **Run the checks below and the self-check at the end of each reference** before delivering.

## Construction errors to screen for

| Error | How to recognise it in your draft | Self-check |
|---|---|---|
| Priority right stretched beyond the price | Its scope is "the above requests" or "all sums", sweeping in interest or losses | Name the price figure as its scope |
| Priority right hedged or conceded | A request dropped, conditioned, or followed by a remark that the term may have run | Make the request; lapse is the defendant's point |
| Settlement defence left unanswered | The employer's recorded argument (long performance of the side agreement, voluntary signature, its own audit) is not named | Name each recorded argument and answer it once |
| Quality objections answered in bulk | "Quality objections are barred" with no defect named | Classify each named defect into its group, with the reason |
| Disputed visas not identified | The complaint says visas bind without saying which ones the employer disputes and why | Identify the disputed set and the ground, then answer |
| Adjustment without direction | "Adjust the unit price" with no upward or downward | State the direction and the remaining quantity |
| Valuation route in the request | A code, a clause, or "to be fixed by appraisal" inside the request line | Move it to the facts; leave a bare marker |
| Planned start taken from an event | The delay count starts at a ceremony or site entry instead of the contract term | Take the planned start from the contract term |
| Interest start on a convenience | Interest from a demand letter, the end of negotiations, or a handover date | Start the day after the payable date or the effective date of termination |
| Adverse clause invoked | A clause that cuts down a request is cited and the request reduced | Leave it to the defendant |
| Loss claimed as price after termination | Costs cut off by a wrongful termination claimed under the payment clauses | Claim them as losses |
| Listing instead of concluding | One sentence holding every stoppage date or visa number, or a delay table | Keep the conclusion and the key milestones |

## Self-check before delivering

1. The document follows the skeleton, ends with the closing and the annex, and contains nothing after
   them; nothing from the "never in the filing" list appears anywhere.
2. Every request is operative only, in the required order, with a bare marker where a value is missing,
   and asks for an order the court must make.
3. The price, its settlement basis, its payable date, and its interest are consistent with one another;
   the priority request covers the price only.
4. Every defence the inputs record against the plaintiff's position is named and answered once; no
   other defence is answered.
5. Every defendant owes a request under a stated rule; the court is the one the forum order reaches,
   checked for specialised and exclusive jurisdiction.
6. Every rule is cited with its article number beside the proposition it carries, and is current law.
7. Every figure, rate, date, and period traces to an input or to a derivation stated next to it.
