# Construction price, settlement basis, interest, quality objections, and the priority right

Apply this when the complaint recovers a construction, engineering, or EPC price for a contractor,
subcontractor, or designer-builder. Each section gives the trigger, the operation, the check, and when
not to use it. The rules are stated by family; in the complaint, cite the current provision that
carries each one, with its article number, beside the proposition it carries. The analysis is done in
your notes; the complaint states its result.

## 1. Choose the settlement basis before stating any figure

**Trigger.** The inputs contain more than one document that could fix the price: a tendered and filed
contract, a side agreement or supplement signed around the same time, a unilateral audit or review by
the employer, a submitted final account, a cost consultant's figure.

**Operation.**
1. List each candidate basis with its date, its signatories and seals, and what it changes.
2. Where the project had to be tendered, a later or parallel agreement that changes the price, the
   pricing method, the term, or the quality standard in substance is not the settlement basis; the
   tendered contract is. Say what the side agreement changes and why that change is substantive.
3. Where a side agreement also has a formation defect (signed under pressure, sealed only by a project
   department without the authorised signatory), state the defect and its effect separately. It is an
   additional ground, not a replacement for the first.
4. A unilateral audit by the employer binds the contractor only if the contract says so. Otherwise it
   is the employer's position.

**Check.** The unpaid price in your request is the governing total minus documented payments, and you
can name the document for each.

**Not applicable** when there is a single signed contract and an agreed final account; then take the
account and move on.

## 2. Answering the defences usually raised against the settlement basis

**Trigger.** The inputs record that the employer has already argued (in a letter, a meeting record, an
earlier pleading, or the defendant's column of a neutral summary) that the side agreement governs.

**Operation.** Name each argument the inputs record and answer it in one or two sentences next to the
price request. The usual ones:
- *The parties performed the side agreement for a long time, so it became a valid variation.* Long
  performance, payments made under it, or the contractor's silence do not turn a substantive deviation
  from a tendered contract into a valid variation: the rule that the tendered contract governs is
  mandatory and cannot be waived by conduct. A genuine variation is one caused by a change in objective
  circumstances not foreseeable at tender (a design change, a change of scope), not one that alters the
  bargain itself.
- *The contractor signed the side agreement voluntarily.* Consent does not cure a substantive deviation
  from a tendered contract; any defect of consent is the additional ground of section 1 step 3.
- *The employer's audit is final.* See section 1 step 4.

**Check.** Every argument the inputs attribute to the employer on the settlement basis is named and
answered once; no argument the inputs do not record is answered.

## 3. Unpaid price, and the date it became payable

**Trigger.** Any claim for unpaid price, and any interest or priority request that hangs on it.

**Operation.** Fix the payable date in this order: the date the contract fixes for payment (often a
period running from submission or review of the final account; read the clause and count); failing
that, the date the works were delivered or taken into use; failing that, the date the settlement
documents were submitted; failing all of these, the date of filing. A contractual payment period counts
only if its trigger actually happened: when the period runs from an agreed account or an issued payment
certificate and the account is disputed or the certificate was never issued, the contractual date
cannot be fixed and you move down the ladder. Record in your notes which rung you used and the document
that dates it. The same date starts interest on the price and anchors the priority term
(section 7).

**Check.** Your interest start date and your priority analysis refer to the same payable date, and the
rung you used has a document behind it.

## 4. Interest on the price

**Operation.**
- A price that is due and unpaid carries a request for interest; make it unless the client has said
  otherwise.
- **Base.** The unpaid price, or the category the documents make overdue. Not damages, not a penalty,
  not an instalment already inside the requested price.
- **Rate standard.** An agreed late payment rate if a document in the inputs shows one; otherwise the
  market lending reference rate published for the period, without a multiplier unless a clause or rule
  supplies one.
- **Start.** The day after the payable date of section 3. A start anchored on a later convenience (the
  breakdown of negotiations, a demand letter) gives away interest.
- **End.** Until actual payment.
- A provisional interest figure to a stated date is included only if it can be computed from values in
  the inputs, or taken from your own side's analysis after checking its base, rate source, and dates;
  label it provisional. Otherwise state the standard with a bare marker for the figure.

## 5. Taking the works into use before acceptance

**Trigger.** The employer occupied, delivered to buyers, opened to traffic, or otherwise used the works
before completion acceptance.

**Operation.**
- The completion date becomes the date possession passed. State that date and its document.
- Having used the works, the employer can no longer object to the quality of the parts it used, except
  for defects in the foundations and the main structure, for which the contractor remains liable for
  the design life (section 6).
- The consequence usually carries into the delay dispute (the completion date stops the delay clock)
  and into the payable date (section 3).

**Not applicable** when the employer's use was of a part the contractor had already handed over under a
signed partial acceptance; then that acceptance governs.

## 6. Quality objections and set-off

**Trigger.** The employer seeks to deduct repair costs, withholds payment for defects, or has listed
defects in a letter or meeting record.

**Operation.**
1. In your notes, list each defect the employer has named, with the document where it named it.
2. Put each defect in exactly one group, and say so by name in the complaint:
   - **Barred by use.** Defects in parts the employer used before acceptance that are not foundations
     or main structure. Foundations and main structure mean the load-bearing system: foundations and
     piles, the structural frame, load-bearing walls, columns, beams, and floor slabs. Seepage and
     waterproofing, finishes, doors and windows, building services, and external works fall outside it
     unless the employer shows that the defect originates in the load-bearing system.
   - **Unproved.** Defects with no joint inspection, no appraisal, and no record other than the
     employer's own statement.
   - **Established.** Defects a joint record or appraisal confirms; answer these on their cost and on
     the retention or warranty mechanism the contract provides.
3. Write one sentence per group: which defects, which group, why, and the rule.

**Check.** Every defect the employer named is classified by name; no defect is answered by a general
statement that "quality objections are barred".

## 7. The contractor's priority right in the works

**Trigger.** Unpaid construction price on works that can be sold or auctioned.

**Operation.**
- The right secures the construction price only. It does not extend to interest, damages, penalties,
  or losses. Word the request so that its scope is the price figure, and do not define it by reference
  to requests that include interest or damages.
- The term is counted from the payable date fixed in section 3. When the payable date could not be fixed
  because the settlement was in dispute, the later rungs apply, and the complaint says only that the
  right is exercised within the term from the payable date it relies on.
- Make the request whenever there is unpaid construction price. Do not drop it, condition it, or narrow
  it because your own count suggests the term may have run: that count may rest on a filing date you do
  not know, and lapse is the defendant's point to raise. The clock of your environment is not the filing
  date.
- Where the employer has already argued in writing that the term ran from an earlier date (for example
  the date of use), answer it by showing why that date was not the payable date.
- The current rule on the term is in the current judicial interpretation on construction contracts;
  a shorter term from a superseded reply is not the law.

**Not applicable** when the claimant did not contract with the employer for the works, or the claim is
not for construction price.

## 8. Self-check

1. The settlement basis is chosen with a stated reason, and every recorded defence against it is
   answered.
2. One payable date is fixed, with its rung and document, and drives both interest and the priority
   term.
3. The price carries an interest request with base, standard, start, and end.
4. Every named quality objection is classified by name into one group with its reason.
5. The priority request's scope is the price only, and it is made without condition or concession.
