# Document form, section content, and length discipline

Apply this to every complaint, before writing the final text. It fixes the order of the parts, what
each part carries, what it must not carry, and how long each part should run. The skeleton is a
general form; every name, figure, date, clause, and court in a real complaint comes from the inputs.

## 1. Skeleton, in order

1. **Title.** `民事起诉状`, centred, alone on its line.
2. **Parties.** Each plaintiff first, then each defendant, numbered when there is more than one on a
   side. After the parties, an agent line only if the inputs name one.
   - A natural person: name, sex, date of birth, ethnicity, domicile, identity number, contact.
   - An organisation: name, domicile, unified social credit code, legal representative or person in
     charge and position, contact.
   - Each element the inputs state (particulars, agent, address, a case number if any) is written as
     the inputs give it, including an element found only in a stamp, a letterhead, a text box, or an
     evidence list; masked values stay masked; only an element the inputs truly do not contain carries
     a short to-be-supplied marker. Nothing else goes in the party block: no role explanation, no note
     on why another person is not sued, no court line.
3. **Cause of action.** One line, only if you state it; the name of the legal relation the main
   request rests on.
4. **Requests.** Heading `诉讼请求`, then numbered requests in this order: any termination or other
   declaration that the money requests presuppose; the principal sums; interest, penalties, and
   charges on them; accessory relief (a priority or security right, liability of another person);
   costs last.
5. **Facts and reasons.** Heading `事实与理由`. First the facts once, in date order; then the argument
   for each request in request order, each rule cited with its article number beside its proposition.
   End with one closing sentence asking the court to grant the requests.
6. **Closing.** On separate lines, in this order and with nothing between them: `此致`; the full name
   of the addressee court; the signature line for the plaintiff (`具状人`), with seal or signature
   space; the date.
7. **Annex.** After the date, a line `附：` followed by the number of copies of the complaint for
   service and the evidence list by item name. No proof purposes, no commentary.

Nothing follows the annex. There is no court line in the caption, no forum section anywhere, and no
note after the date.

## 2. What each part says and does not say

| Part | Says | Does not say |
|---|---|---|
| Parties | The particulars the inputs give, or a marker | Why someone is or is not a party; the court; the source of a particular |
| Requests | Orders the court must make and the inputs support: payer, payee, object, amount or base, rate standard, period, direction | The rule, the valuation route, the derivation, the reason a value is missing, a method of fixing it, "subject to the court finding", alternative or conditional wording, a reservation of rights, a list of heads not claimed; a confirmation of a matter that only supports a reason |
| Facts | Conclusions and key milestones in date order with their dates | Events no document shows; the other side's figures as facts; the file's gaps; long quotations; every date, instruction, or document number strung into one sentence |
| Reasons | For each request: the facts that meet its prerequisites, the cited rule, the conclusion; a short answer to a defence the defendant has already raised | Forum or arbitration analysis; answers to defences not raised; "even if" fallbacks; concessions; a list or section of claims not pursued; procedural applications; the evidence list retold |
| Closing and annex | The fixed lines above | Remarks, reservations, explanations |

A value awaiting appraisal or calculation is written as subject to the appraisal result or to
calculation at judgment, once, in the facts and reasons. A right the plaintiff keeps for later is at
most one sentence at the end of the facts and reasons, and never appears in the requests.

## 3. Never in the filing

Each of these belongs in your working notes or nowhere. Search the final text for each before
delivering.

1. Drafting notes: what the drafter assumed, what the file lacks, what the inputs do or do not
   contain, how the document was prepared, words addressed to the drafter or the client.
2. A forum, jurisdiction, or arbitration section, a caption line naming the court, or a note beside
   the court's name.
3. A concession against the plaintiff: that a right has or may have lapsed, that a period has run,
   that a claim is dropped or not pursued, that a clause favouring the defendant applies, that the
   plaintiff's own evidence contains an error, that a head overlaps another and is therefore reduced.
4. A procedural application: appraisal, preservation, an order to produce documents, a court
   investigation.
5. A rebuttal of a defence the defendant has not raised, or an "even if" argument.
6. A statement of why a person is not sued or why a grievance is not claimed.
7. Events or attitudes no document shows: repeated demands, failed negotiations, a claim that the
   defendant never objected, the defendant acknowledging the debt, completion or acceptance of the
   works, a payment tied to a clause no document ties it to.
8. An assertion about what a contract provides or omits when the contract text is not in the inputs.
9. Clauses or statutes quoted at length, and the evidence list retold with proof purposes.

## 4. Length discipline

Do:
- Write each fact once, in the chronological narrative, and refer back to it when arguing.
- Give each derivation as one sentence: base, count or rate, result.
- Answer a defence the defendant has raised in one or two sentences with the rule that defeats it.
- State a clause's effect in your own words in a clause-length sentence.
- Keep each request to its operative content.

Do not:
- Reproduce a table, a computation sheet, or a demand letter's figures line by line; state the clause
  and the result.
- Retell each piece of evidence or attach proof purposes to it in the body.
- Quote contract clauses or statutes at length.
- Write a payment, delay, or treatment table, or pack every date, instruction, or document number into
  one long sentence; the working stays in your notes, and the complaint states the result and the key
  milestones.
- Add sections the skeleton does not have (forum, background of the parties, claims not pursued,
  explanation of method, notes).

## 5. Self-check

1. Parts appear in the skeleton order, and the closing lines are consecutive; every caption and closing
   field the inputs state is filled in.
2. The annex is last and lists copies and evidence names only.
3. No part contains anything from its "does not say" column, and none of the nine items in section 3
   appears anywhere.
4. Every sentence of the facts and reasons either states a documented fact needed by a request,
   applies a rule to it, or answers a raised defence.
