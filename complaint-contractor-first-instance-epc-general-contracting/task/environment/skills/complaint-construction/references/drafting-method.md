# Drafting method common to every complaint

Apply this to every complaint. It gives the general steps from the inputs to the filed text. The
direction references of this skill add the specialised operations for the claims they cover; where a
direction reference is more specific, it governs for its claims. All working below is done in your
notes; the complaint carries only its results.

## 1. Sort every source by whose voice it is

Label each source before taking anything from it: a document both parties signed; a record made by a
neutral third party (a court, an agency, a hospital, a bank, a post office); a document one side made
alone; the other side's letter, reply, or defence; a neutral case summary that lists both sides'
positions; your own side's evidence list, narrative, or preliminary analysis.

- A figure or characterisation found only in the other side's documents is their position. Do not
  state it as a fact, and never let it into a request.
- A neutral summary that lists both sides' positions gives you both positions, not a finding. Take your
  side's and give the reason it holds. A position it attributes to the defendant counts as a defence
  already raised.
- A figure your own side's evidence already states (an evidence list total, an allocation, a daily or
  monthly income, a netted balance in a signed document) is adopted as it stands when nothing in the
  inputs contradicts it. Do not recompute it from components, with an allocation key, or with a
  rounding you chose yourself; a figure you rebuilt from an input you invented is itself invented.
- A preliminary claims analysis for your side is a proposal: check its rules, parties, and forum
  against the primary documents, and search the full contract rather than stopping at the clause it
  names. A provisional figure it has computed may be used, marked as provisional, once you have checked
  its base, its rate source, and its dates.
- A reference judgment from another case supplies rates and methods, never its own parties' amounts.

Verification: for each figure and each characterisation you will use, you can name the document and
say whose voice it is.

## 2. Build the claim map (notes only)

One row per grievance the client has or the documents reveal. For each row decide:

1. **The remedy type** the law gives for it. A grievance framed as lost profit, unfairness, or extra
   cost is often a different remedy in law; the request names that remedy.
2. **The prerequisites of that remedy**, and whether each is satisfied or must itself be requested.
   Damages for loss of the bargain while the contract is still on foot, or interest before the sum was
   due, cannot be granted as written.
3. **The amount, or the base and standard** when the amount cannot be fixed (section 6).
4. **The defendant or defendants** who owe it (section 5).

Request only what needs an order of the court and what the inputs support. A matter that serves only to
support a reason or to answer a defence is argued in the facts and reasons and is not made a separate
request, declaration, or confirmation. Every grievance becomes a request, part of the reasons for one,
or is dropped; none of these choices is explained in the complaint.

A principal sum you request that is owed and overdue, or money that must be returned, normally carries
a companion request for interest or a charge for the use of money; decide it on the map rather than
leaving it out by default. Interest attaches to the sum requested: do not add a second interest request
on an instalment or component already inside a larger requested sum.

Do not add a request because a remedy exists in the abstract: a request against a person whose
liability no document shows, a declaration nobody disputes, or a confirmation whose only use is to
rebut an objection is an unsupported addition.

## 3. Write the requests

Each request is one order the court can make: who pays whom, what, how much or on what base, at what
rate, from when to when, and, for an adjustment, in which direction. Use the order in
`references/document-form-and-length.md` section 1.

The request line holds only operative content. The derivation, the rule, the valuation route, the
reason a value is missing, and any method of fixing it go in the facts and reasons or nowhere. When a
value is unavailable, the line carries a short to-be-supplied marker and nothing more. When a later
request refers back to earlier ones, check that every request it sweeps in belongs to the base it
describes.

Verification: read each request alone. Payer, payee, object, amount or base, rate standard, period, and
direction are present or replaced by a bare marker, and the line contains no reasoning.

## 4. Write the facts and reasons

First tell the facts once in date order: formation, performance, the events that made each sum due or
caused each loss, demand, non-payment or breach. State conclusions and key milestones. Then argue the
requests in the order they were made, each with the rule that turns those facts into the remedy.

Cite each rule by instrument and article number, beside the proposition it carries, and say what it
does there. Use current law only: the pre-codification contract statute, the general principles
statute, and superseded judicial interpretations are not a basis for a claim. A number that does not
match the rule is an error, so check each number against the article's content; the answer to doubt is
to verify, not to drop the number. A block of provisions listed at the end supports nothing.

Where a result depends on a figure, a date, or a period, give the base, the count, and the result in a
sentence, not the working. Where it depends on a clause, name the clause, state its effect in your own
words, and apply it.

When the inputs show a defence the defendant has already put forward (a reply letter, a defence in
earlier proceedings, an objection to the account, a position a neutral summary attributes to it), name
the defence and answer it in one or two sentences, next to the request it threatens. A payment
condition inside the defendant's own undertaking is not a defence it has raised. Do not answer any
other defence.

Verification: each request has a passage that states its prerequisites as facts, applies a cited rule,
and reaches that request's conclusion.

## 5. Parties, court, fields, and dates

- **Plaintiff.** The person who holds the claim now, in the direction the instruction gives.
- **Defendants.** Go request by request and name who owes it under a rule. Leave out persons who only
  appear in the facts; leaving them out is silent.
- **Court.** Work through the forum questions in your notes in this order and stop at the first that
  applies: a binding arbitration clause between these parties on this relation; a specialised court for
  this kind of dispute; exclusive jurisdiction; a written forum clause with an actual connection;
  otherwise the defendant's domicile or the place the rules make relevant; then the level. The result
  appears only as the court named after `此致`. If the inputs identify the court only by a connecting
  place, write the court of that place at the level the rules give, with a bare marker for any missing
  part of the name.
- **Fields.** Every caption and closing field the inputs state is written exactly as given. Only a field
  the inputs truly do not contain carries a short to-be-supplied marker.
- **Dates.** An input that places an event in a month, at month end, or before a stated day lets you fix
  a day: take the last day the record allows, count from it, and state the result as the plaintiff's
  position. Mark a date as to be supplied only when no input gives it at any precision. The filing date
  is the date the inputs give for the complaint; if none, leave the closing date blank for signature.
  The clock of the environment you are running in is never the filing date and never a basis for
  concluding that a period has run.

## 6. Figures, periods, and totals

- **Competing figures.** List each candidate figure for the same quantity with its document, its voice,
  and what it includes; decide which governs and why (section 1).
- **Payments.** Do not assign a payment to a clause or an instalment unless a document does so. If the
  documents give the total paid, use the total.
- **Values the inputs do not give.** A rate for the period, a margin, a market price, an appraisal
  result: the request states the base and standard with a bare marker. Never supply a value from
  memory, never apply an assumed percentage or margin, and never round a standard taken from a source.
- **Periods.** Write both dates and the count, and recompute the count yourself. When a period runs
  from an event plus days or months, state the event, its date, the length, and the end date.
- **Totals.** A total is the sum of its items and nothing else. Recompute it; when a request states a
  total of several heads, the facts show each head and the heads add to it.

## 7. Reasoning errors to screen for

| Error | How to recognise it in your draft | Self-check |
|---|---|---|
| Adopting the other side's position | A figure, date, or characterisation traceable only to the defendant's documents | Trace it (section 1); answer it or delete it |
| Rebuilding your own side's figure | A total, allocation, or daily figure recomputed although your own evidence states it | Adopt the stated figure unless an input contradicts it |
| A conclusion the numbers do not support | A total that is not the sum of its items; a count the two dates do not give | Recompute from the stated inputs |
| A remedy without its prerequisite | Damages for loss of the bargain with no termination; interest before the due date | Name the legal state the request presupposes and what establishes it |
| Inventing a value | A percentage, daily standard, market rate, or allocation key no input contains | Take it from an input or mark it |
| Inventing facts or history | See `references/document-form-and-length.md` section 3 item 7 | Every event has a document; delete what does not |
| Hedging or conceding | A conditioned request, a request dropped because a value is missing, a lapse conceded | Sue on documented relations; mark the value; leave lapse to the defendant |
| Writing notes into the filing | A forum section, a remark on the file, a list of claims not pursued, an application | Move it to your notes |
| A request that only carries a reason | A confirmation no order needs; interest on a component already inside a requested sum | Argue it in the reasons; delete the request |
| A gap marker where a date can be fixed | "To be supplied" beside an event the inputs date to a month or month end | Take the last day the record allows and count |

## 8. Stop conditions

- If two defensible positions exist and nothing decides between them, choose the one your side's
  documents support best and argue it; do not present the alternative.
- If a request would need a legal relation no document shows, leave the request out entirely; do not
  hedge it and do not explain its absence.
- If a value is missing, do not stop and do not guess: draft the request with its base and standard and
  a bare marker.
