# Filing baseline for every evidence opinion

This reference is the frame every evidence opinion filed with a court or arbitral tribunal shares,
whichever side of the evidence you are on. The direction method reference says what goes inside
the body and the closing request; this file says what the document around it looks like, what it
never contains, how long it runs, and how legal conclusions are stated.

## 1. Read everything before drafting

Open and read every reference listed in the routing table of the entry point, this one included,
before you write the first line of the draft. Then apply only the parts whose conditions match
your task. Reading is unconditional; applying is conditional.

## 2. The filed document is a filing

The reader is a judge or arbitrator who has the bundle in front of them. They do not need the
bundle described back to them. They need, for each item or group, a position they can act on and
the reason for it. Every sentence speaks to the tribunal for your client.

The document has, in this order: a header, an opening, the body set out in the direction method
reference, one closing request, and a sign-off. Nothing else.

## 3. What the filed document never contains

- **Drafting notes.** Remarks on how the inputs were prepared (scans, transcriptions, anonymised
  names, placeholders, approximate figures), references to internal working papers or to chapters
  of a case file, remarks on inconsistent numbering, and comments on what the inputs failed to
  contain.
- **Concessions against your client.** Calling one of your own elements weak, thin, pending or not
  yet proved; accepting as true what a document the other side made alone asserts; giving up a
  claim, a right or an argument; disputing a figure or a finding that favours your client; using
  your own side's internal assessments, fallback figures or settlement ranges as a figure.
- **Procedural applications nobody asked for.** Orders to produce an original, to supplement, to
  explain or verify; requests for appraisal, investigation, collection of evidence or witness
  summons, including conditional ones ("if the tribunal considers it necessary"). Where an item is
  defective, say what the defect stops it from proving. That is the whole remedy inside this
  document.
- **Extra chapters.** Any chapter the direction method reference does not call for: a chapter that
  regroups the item or group opinions by fact, a chapter of favourable facts, a separate chapter of
  conclusions ahead of the request, a merits argument after the evidence opinions. Chain
  conclusions and favourable facts go under the item or group they belong to.
- **States of mind the documents do not show.** Do not turn conduct into knowledge, acceptance,
  motive or bad faith. Describe the conduct the documents record and state its legal effect.
- **A request for a substantive judgment**, unless the direction method reference allows one.

Counterexample: when the instruction itself asks for an application, a synthesis chapter or a
merits argument, write it in its own section after the body.

## 4. Legal conclusions carry their authority

Evaluating evidence often requires a legal conclusion: whether a term binds a party, who carries
the burden on an assertion, whether a prior decision precludes this claim, whether a document
satisfies a formal requirement, whether a period has run. Draw that conclusion; do not defer it as
a matter for the court. State it with the rule it rests on, naming the statute or judicial
interpretation and the article number, and do not quote the article's text. Cite the provisions
practitioners routinely cite for that proposition.

- Never invent an article number. If you cannot place the rule, state the rule and name the
  instrument only.
- Cite a provision only where it decides a point. Do not cite a provision for the act of filing
  the opinion itself.
- Each conclusion and its rule sit in the same passage as the item or group it decides.

## 5. Document frame

- **Header.** Title of the document; case number line; addressee tribunal; the party lines, each
  with the party's procedural status, and for an entity its domicile and legal representative when
  the inputs give them. Label each party with the procedural status the instruction and the case
  materials use for it, such as `原告：××` or `被告：××`. Do not coin a combined label that joins
  a role in this document to that status. The direction method reference says which parties to
  list.
- **Opening.** A short preamble or a general statement, as the direction method reference sets out.
  It identifies whose evidence it is, produced with which filing or on what occasion, how many items
  or groups, and in what capacity your client speaks. It never restates the evidence list.
- **Body and closing request.** As the direction method reference sets out.
- **Sign-off.** The customary valediction (`此致`), the tribunal's name on the next line, then the
  party's name under the same designation as in the header (with its seal for an entity), the
  authorised agent line with every agent the inputs name, and the date, leaving the day blank if it
  is not fixed.

**Fields come from the inputs.** Every header and sign-off field the inputs state (case number,
parties, statuses, domiciles, legal representatives, authorised agents, addresses, tribunal) is
written out exactly as the inputs give it. Search every part of the inputs for them: the party
table, powers of attorney, the other side's filings, cover letters, court notices, not only the
first page. An agent named anywhere in the inputs for your client appears on the agent line. Only
a field the inputs really do not contain is left as an empty field; do not fill it with a plausible
value, and do not explain its absence.

## 6. Consistency

- One stance per document, everywhere it appears.
- Attribute quoted content to whoever said it: a party's argument recorded in a ruling is not the
  ruling's finding.
- Do not let a later passage rely on an input an earlier passage rejected.

## 7. Length discipline

Do:

- Follow the source numbering and give each item or group one opinion, placed where it bites.
- Keep the opinion on an item nobody could contest short; short is not absent, and the direction
  method reference says what every opinion must still contain.
- Spend the full reasoning on the items or groups with a real defect and on those the case turns on.
- State a point shared by several items once, where the items sit together.
- Quote a document only in a short phrase inside quotation marks; otherwise describe it in your own
  words.

Do not:

- Restate the evidence list, the stated proof purposes in full, or long passages of any document.
- Analyse each attachment of an item separately unless they prove different things.
- Add summary tables, indexes, chronologies or calculation tables that repeat the text.
- Add any section the frame and the direction method reference do not call for.
- Carry reasoning about the merits beyond what deciding the evidence requires.

## 8. General stop conditions

- **Outside your remit.** If the instruction limits you to one bundle, do not comment on other
  bundles, and never write in another party's voice.
- **Undecidable point.** If the inputs do not let you decide a point, state the narrower conclusion
  they do support. Do not request more material and do not flag the point as open in the filing.
- **Transcription.** A section that could be produced by copying the bundle's columns carries no
  opinion; rewrite it as a position with a reason.
