# Burden on the defences, the holding, and the request

Apply this whenever the deliverable presents your own client's evidence. It covers the three
closing moves: the burden section, the holding on the standard of proof, and the request.

## 1. The burden section

**Build the defence list first, in your notes.** Take every defence the other side has raised in
its filings, and every defence its own documents foreseeably support (for example payment or
discharge, set off, defective performance, a term excluding or limiting liability, a time bar, lack
of standing). One row per defence; do not merge two defences into one row.

**Allocate.** A party asserting a fact that would defeat, reduce or discharge the claim carries the
burden of proving it. For each row, write: the defence, the party that asserts it, that the burden
is on that party, what that party has produced, and the consequence (not proved, therefore the
defence fails). "Nothing has been produced" is worth stating where it is true.

**Write it in one place, compactly.** The burden section is its own section after the relationship
between the groups. Write it either as one table with one row per defence, or as one sentence per
defence in a single passage. Name the rule allocating the burden once, with its article number, at
the head of the section (or in the general statement, and refer to it here); do not repeat it in
every row, and do not spread the burden analysis across the groups.

Verification: every defence on your list has a row, and each row names the party bearing the
burden and what it has produced.

Counterexamples:

- A defence that only denies an element of your claim (the other side says the work was not done)
  does not shift the burden; it is answered by the group that proves the element, not in this
  section.
- Do not rehearse the other side's arguments at length; one clause names the defence.

## 2. The holding on the standard of proof

End the general statement, or put just before the request, one affirmative sentence: the evidence
filed establishes the elements your client relies on to the standard of proof that applies in
civil cases, naming those elements in the same sentence (for example, the relationship, the
claimant's status, performance, the amount and each respondent's liability), with the rule on the
standard.

- Name only the elements the bundle does carry. An element that is not carried is left out of the
  holding and out of the request, and the gap stays in your working notes.
- Never grade your own elements in the filing: no "weakest", "not yet fully proved", "pending",
  "relatively thin", and no statement of what would still be needed.

## 3. The request

The request is the last section before the sign-off. It is a short numbered list, one sentence per
item, and it is built from the groups, not written freehand.

**Build it.**

1. First item: that the evidence filed (by number range) be admitted.
2. Then one item for each determination a group carries, in the order of the groups: the
   characterisation of the relationship and, where in issue, its validity or invalidity; your
   client's status or capacity; the basis on which the measure is computed and the resulting sum;
   the balance owed after payments and any set off you accept.
3. Then the liability of each respondent, in the order of liability: the primary obligor first,
   then any party whose liability is joint, secondary or limited, stating the limit of that
   liability as the governing rule or the documents fix it.
4. Then any determination the pleaded claims seek that attaches a statutory protection or priority
   to a class of claim or of persons, where a group proves the facts that trigger it.

**Match it.** Every request traces to a group whose conclusion supports it, and every group whose
conclusion is a determination the tribunal must make has a request. Every sum in the request
equals the sum derived in the text. Ask only for heads of claim already pleaded; do not add a new
head, a new sum, or an alternative the pleadings do not contain.

**Keep it clean.** No procedural applications (production, collection, appraisal, verification,
witness summons, a date for further evidence). No argument inside the request; the reasons are in
the groups. No conditional requests ("if the tribunal finds otherwise").

Verification: put the request beside the list of groups. Each request points to a group; each
determinative group points to a request; the order runs from admission to the liability of each
respondent.

## 4. Stop conditions

- If a group's element is not carried, drop its request rather than asking for it with a
  qualification.
- Do not ask for a determination about a party that is not before the tribunal.
