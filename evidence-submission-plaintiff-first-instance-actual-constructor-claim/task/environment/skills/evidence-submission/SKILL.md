# Evidence submission opinions

You act for the party filing the evidence. The tribunal must be able to read, in one document, which
element of your client's claims each group of evidence proves, which group everything else rests
on, where independent items confirm one another, why the other side's defences are its burden, and
exactly what your client asks the tribunal to find. You do not examine your own evidence the way you
would examine the other side's.

## Before drafting: read every reference

Open and read every reference listed in "Reference routing" at the end of this file before you write
the first line of the draft, whatever the bundle contains. Then apply only the parts whose
conditions match your task. Reading is unconditional; applying is conditional.

## Step 1: build the element map and the evidence inventory, in your working notes

**Element map.** From the pleaded claims, list every element the tribunal must find for each claim:
the legal relationship and its characterisation, your client's status or capacity, performance,
the basis on which the measure is computed, the amount and what has been paid, each respondent's
liability and its kind (primary, joint, secondary, limited), any time bar, and every defence the
other side has raised or will foreseeably raise.

**Evidence inventory.** One row per item: number, name, who made it (your client, the other side, a
third party, a public body), and its stated proof purpose.

Verification: every element has at least one item, and every item serves an element. An item with
no element is left in its group without comment; an element with no item stays out of the holding
and out of the request.

## Step 2: group, find the base group, and order the groups

Apply `references/grouping-and-corroboration.md` sections 1 to 3. Each group carries one element.
Decide which group every other group depends on, put it where the dependency requires, and say so
in the general statement.

## Step 3: write each group as your own proposition

Apply `references/grouping-and-corroboration.md` sections 4 and 5: the group proposition in your own
prose, a short group conclusion, no admissibility verdicts on your own items, no item by item
limits.

## Step 4: connect the groups

Apply `references/grouping-and-corroboration.md` section 6: name the pairs of items from different
groups that confirm the same dimension of the same fact.

## Step 5: burden, holding and request

Apply `references/burden-holding-and-request.md`: the burden section, one row per defence; the
holding sentence naming the elements carried; the request set built from the groups.

## Step 6: figures, measures and periods

When a group proposition or a request turns on an amount, a pricing basis, a payment, a set off, a
period or the order of events, apply `references/figures-periods-and-limitation.md` before writing
the sentence that uses it.

## Step 7: write the document

Use the frame in `references/filing-baseline.md` and the section order in
`references/grouping-and-corroboration.md` section 1.

## Self-check before delivering

1. Sections run: header, general statement, groups, relationship between groups, burden, request,
   sign-off; nothing else.
2. The header lists every party and its procedural status, and the addressee tribunal; the sign-off
   names the filing party, every authorised agent the inputs name, and the date.
3. The general statement names the base group and says which groups depend on it.
4. Each group has its own proposition and conclusion; no item receives a stance on genuineness,
   lawfulness or relevance.
5. Each corroboration pair names two items from different groups or sources, the one dimension they
   share, and that they agree.
6. The burden section has one row per defence, each with the party bearing it, the rule and what
   that party has produced.
7. The holding sentence names the elements it covers; nothing calls any element weak or pending.
8. Every request traces to a group, every group that ends in a determination has a request, and the
   order runs from admission of the evidence to the liability of each respondent, primary before
   secondary or limited.
9. Every figure traces to a document or a derivation beside it.
10. Nothing from the list in `references/filing-baseline.md` section 3 is present.
11. You read all four references before drafting.

## Failure modes

- **Cross examining yourself.** Stating that your own item is genuine or "not denied" reads as a
  concession and adds nothing.
- **Item by item analysis.** Writing what each item proves and does not prove multiplies the length
  and hides the group proposition.
- **Wrong base group.** Naming the group with the hardest legal question as decisive, rather than
  the group the others depend on.
- **Cross dimension pairing.** Pairing two records of different dimensions of a fact and calling it
  corroboration.
- **Self assessment.** Grading your own elements as strong or weak in the filing.
- **Requests without a group, or groups without a request.**
