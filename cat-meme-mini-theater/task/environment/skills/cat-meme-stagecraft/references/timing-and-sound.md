# Timing, dialogue, and sound

## Make the reveal readable

A useful comic beat has an expectation, a reveal, and a response. Their boundaries need not coincide with scene changes. Make the decisive information intelligible before, or clearly together with, the reaction. There is no mandatory gap: a simultaneous graphic and expression can work when their connection is immediate. Avoid revealing the punchline early in a caption, label, or background graphic.

Vary pacing by dramatic purpose. Establishing shots orient the viewer; exchanges carry information; reaction shots can use a pause, cut, push-in, or hold. Repeating the same duration or animation on every shot weakens the distinction. Use a callback to transform an earlier line or expectation rather than simply repeating it.

Keep captions short enough for their display interval and readable at the final viewing size. Separate speaker identity from dialogue when the cast is ambiguous. Check line breaks and margins in rendered frames, not merely in a text editor. A caption can remain over a cut if its speaker and timing stay clear.

## Coordinate the attention changes

Mark the moments that matter within each shot: information appears, reading completes, the performer reaches the useful expression, and the scene settles or cuts. These are editing decisions, not equal subdivisions. If a list builds, leave its consequential item enough exposure to register. If a reaction becomes the focus, reduce competing motion elsewhere.

For a repeated-work montage, coordinate the activity, clock or environmental changes, and task-status graphics. A progress cycle can reset when a new attempt begins; unrelated resets look like implementation artifacts. Check the montage as a short sequence rather than judging each overlay in isolation.

Hold the ending long enough to connect the reversal to the opening expectation. Silence, stillness, or an ellipsis can provide the response when justified by the script. Do not consume that interval with an elaborate outgoing transition.

## Mix performances deliberately

Original meme clips may contain cries, speech, music, noise, or silence. Preserve the brief's required original role voices and their narrative assignments. Extracting and routing those chosen excerpts separately while muting duplicate video audio avoids accidental overlap when several performers appear together. Other music and effects may be designed for the story; source-voice preservation takes priority over general mixing advice.

Give the important cue acoustic space. Reduce background music under dialogue-like sounds, avoid stacking several loud reactions, and consider a brief music interruption when it supports the reveal. Shape edits with short fades to avoid clicks. A held frame does not require held audio; a repeated action does not require a repeated sound.

Measure the final mix, not just each input. Check clipping, abrupt changes, missing audio, and excessive silence. Loudness measurements describe the signal but cannot determine whether a joke lands or a source sound is distracting. Record that limitation if listening is unavailable.

## Revision checks

When extending a reaction or moving a reveal, inspect the adjacent caption boundaries and audio cues as well as the shot duration. Confirm that the final scene still has time to resolve and that the render's duration and edit plan match the new timeline.
