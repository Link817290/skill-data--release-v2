# Cat Meme Stagecraft

Treat the screenplay as a sequence of changes the viewer must understand. Dialogue supplies meaning; performances, staging, and changing visual states make that meaning observable. Use the supplied brief to determine requirements and this skill to decide how to realize them. Preserve the requested language and delivery format.

## Translate the screenplay into screen events

Before choosing footage, separate each passage into spoken information, visible action or state, emotional attitude, and its causal connection to the next passage. Distinguish characters in the physical scene from internal reactions and imagined cutaways. A screenplay paragraph is not necessarily one shot.

For a new film or a structural revision, read [script-to-shot-plan.md](references/script-to-shot-plan.md). Build a compact working breakdown that connects each required event to a planned screen moment. Keep design choices separate from requirements: shot sizes and transitions may change, but a specified action should not disappear into a caption. Do not turn this working breakdown into an additional deliverable unless requested.

## Cast the action and attitude

Use filenames and metadata to narrow the pool, then inspect candidate performances. Select against the combination of posture, activity, interaction, emotional intensity, and usable motion. A generic emotion tag is insufficient when the script calls for a specific performance.

Read [casting-and-performance.md](references/casting-and-performance.md) when choosing or replacing footage. Establish stable identities for physical roles and a clear convention for internal reactions. Once a match is established, reuse it deliberately; a technically cleaner but behaviorally different performer can change the scene's meaning. Optional props should support the scene rather than demonstrate asset coverage.

## Build the sequence around changes

Arrange expectation, new information, response, escalation, and payoff according to the story. Give each shot a purpose and an identifiable change in audience knowledge or emotion. Track time, task status, messages, and other recurring visual states across cuts. Repeated actions become a montage when their context develops; repetition alone does not establish progression.

Read [timing-and-sound.md](references/timing-and-sound.md) when setting shot lengths, caption intervals, reaction holds, or audio cues. Let information become intelligible before, or clearly together with, its reaction. Budget reading time and the ending before adding transitions. A graphic reveal, a changing progress display, or a held expression can carry the joke without a new camera angle.

## Implement a coherent visual system

Separate the scene into environment, performers and props, story graphics, and captions. Establish consistent conventions for role labels, messages, thought scenes, and impact text; use changes in scale or motion to mark meaningful escalation. Compose for the requested aspect ratio and preserve visible actions at the final viewing size.

Read [compositing-and-rendering.md](references/compositing-and-rendering.md) when preparing footage or implementing the timeline. Use explicit shot boundaries and local frames, reusable visual components, and deterministic source-time mapping. Inspect the installed Remotion APIs before relying on version-specific examples. Start with readable staging, then refine animation and audio.

## Review the encoded story

Review a contact sheet for story order, role continuity, and visual progression, then inspect motion around gestures, reveals, cuts, and caption changes. For each required event, locate evidence in the exported film: a plan entry or code comment is not screen evidence. Check that internal reactions still refer to the intended character and that captions have not become a substitute for acting.

Probe and fully decode the encoded video, verify dimensions, duration and streams, and inspect frames extracted from that file. Listen to the final mix when playback is available; numerical audio checks alone do not establish appropriate sound. Record any unverified aspect accurately.

Keep project paths relative, dependencies reproducible, and derived assets included or regenerable from supplied sources. Verify the documented render command. Reconcile the delivered edit plan with the finished timeline and actual assets. For a revision, check the changed beats and their adjacent cuts, state changes, captions, and sound cues while preserving unrelated decisions.
