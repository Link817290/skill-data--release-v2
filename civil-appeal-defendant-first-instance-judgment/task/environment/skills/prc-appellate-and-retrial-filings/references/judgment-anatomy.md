# Taking the decision apart

Read this at step 1, before you look for anything to attack. The index you build here is what every
later step is checked against.

## The unit of work is the operative determination

A decision has a narrative part, a reasoning part, and an operative part. Only the operative part
binds. Index that part first, one row per determination, in the decision's own order and using its
own numbering, because every later reference you make to it has to be findable by a reader holding
the decision.

For each row, record five things:

| Field | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Ordered | What the party must do, or what is declared, or what is refused |
| Disposes of | Which pleaded claim, in whole or in part, this answers |
| Chain | The evidence named, the facts found, the rule applied, the arithmetic run |
| Stance | Favourable, adverse, or neutral to your side |
| Delta | Where adverse and quantified, the gap between what was claimed and what was ordered |

The Delta column is where the document's substance usually is. A determination that grants part of a
claim is two facts: what was granted, and what was refused without being listed as a refusal. The
refused part is frequently the largest thing in the case and the easiest to leave out of the index,
because it does not appear as an operative item of its own.

## When the reasoning and the operative part disagree

This happens more often than it should, and when it happens it is one of the strongest available
grounds, because it needs no evidence beyond the decision itself.

Two forms to look for:

- The reasoning accepts a proposition and the operative part proceeds as though it had been rejected,
  or the reverse.
- Two passages of reasoning cannot both be true, and each supports a different determination.

Record these in the index as you find them, against the row they affect. Do not start drafting them
yet; they still have to pass the step 3 threshold, and an internal contradiction that changes nothing
is still not a ground.

## A claim the decision never reached

Compare the index against the pleaded claims. A claim that appears in no row was not decided. That
is a different defect from a claim decided wrongly, it usually has a different remedy, and it is
found only by running the comparison deliberately. The decision will not flag it.

## When there is a decision behind the decision

Where what you attack is itself a review of an earlier decision, index both, and align them row by
row. Three cases, and they are not interchangeable:

- The later decision adopted the earlier determination. Your ground has to reach the reasoning that
  adopted it, not only the original reasoning, because the adoption is what now stands.
- The later decision replaced it. The earlier reasoning is history and attacking it wastes the
  reader's attention.
- The later decision is silent on it while changing something it depended on. This is a live gap and
  it is worth indexing separately.

Where the relief you seek requires both decisions to be undone, both have to be named in the relief.
A request that removes only the later one leaves the earlier one standing and gives you nothing.

## Verification before you leave this step

- Every operative item is in the index, including refusals expressed as a partial grant.
- Every pleaded claim maps to an item, or is recorded as not reached.
- Every adverse row has a Delta, or a note saying why it is not quantifiable.
- Nothing in the index is a grievance yet. This step produces a map, not an argument.
