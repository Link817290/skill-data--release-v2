# Cross examination opinions

You act for the party answering the other side's evidence. For every item the other side produced,
the tribunal must be able to read its evidentiary stance and what the item does and does not prove
against the purpose for which it is offered. State and reason every disputed property. Uncontested
genuineness and lawfulness may be stated once for a consecutive group of official instruments when
their common public origin supplies the reason; the item identities and the group's common reason
must remain clear. A formal filing ends with a short treatment request. When the instruction asks
only for a concise opinion and does not require a filing-ready carrier, the final item/group
conclusion followed by the identified party and date is a valid abbreviated disposition.

## Before drafting: read every reference

Open and read every reference listed in "Reference routing" at the end of this file before you write
the first line of the draft, whatever the bundle contains. Then apply only the parts whose
conditions match your task. Reading is unconditional; applying is conditional.

## Step 1: build three inventories, in your working notes

**Evidence inventory.** One row per item, in the other side's numbering: number, name, form
(original, copy, photograph, electronic record, report, official document, contract, a party's own
record), who made it, whether your side signed, received, replied to or acknowledged it, and its
stated proof purpose.

**Ground inventory.** One row per proposition the other side asserts, from every filing in the
inputs and from the proof purposes. A pleading or a procedural objection usually carries several
grounds; split them so that each has its own row.

**Figure and date inventory.** Every figure and date in the other side's own documents that bears on
a ground, with the document it comes from.

Verification: every ground row has a target location in the draft, under the item offered for it. A
ground with no location is an argument about to go unanswered.

Counterexample: if the other side has filed nothing but the bundle, the ground inventory comes only
from the proof purposes; do not invent positions for them.

## Step 2: decide the stance on each property from the document's origin

Apply `references/cross-examination-method.md` sections 2 and 3. The stance on genuineness follows
from who made the document and whether your side can check it, not from whether it hurts you. A
document the other side sent and your side received is genuine; its assertions are not thereby
true. A record nobody outside the other side can check is not accepted. A report the other side
commissioned is accepted as issued and attacked on what it can prove.

Verification: for every item you can say which origin class it belongs to and why.

## Step 3: decide what each item proves against its stated purpose

Name the fact in issue the item is offered for, then state what the item establishes and where it
stops, and why. When the item belongs to a recognisable type (report, a document one party made
alone, copy or electronic record, official record, a form offered in place of a required
instrument, a prior decision, a contract text, a registration record), apply
`references/probative-value-limits.md`. Test chains, favourable facts and the burden on the other
side's assertions as `references/cross-examination-method.md` sections 4 to 7 set out.

Verification: each item opinion carries, in one passage under that item, the object, the limit and
the reason. No conclusion is parked in a preamble, a table or a later chapter.

## Step 4: figures and dates

When an opinion turns on an amount, a count, a period or the order of events, apply
`references/figures-and-timeline.md` before writing the sentence that uses it. In particular, check
whether two figures in the other side's own documents already show that part of your case is not
in dispute.

## Step 5: write the document

Use the frame in `references/filing-baseline.md` and the section order in
`references/cross-examination-method.md` section 1. Use the full header, preamble, two-tier closing
and sign-off when the instruction asks for a filing-ready opinion. For a concise opinion without
that requirement, use a title, an unambiguous evidence-to-
opinion mapping, the item/group opinions in order, and the identified issuing party and date.

## Self-check before delivering

1. Every item in the other side's list is covered, individually or in a consecutive group whose
   items share the same origin, stance and reason, and the items remain in the other side's order.
2. Every disputed stance carries its reason in the same passage. A concise grouped acceptance of
   official court instruments is valid when their identified public origin supplies the common
   reason; a blanket stance over unlike items is not.
3. Every stance on genuineness matches the item's origin class, and is the same everywhere the item
   is used.
4. Every ground in the ground inventory is answered under the item offered for it, with the rule
   that decides it where a legal conclusion is needed.
5. Every figure and count traces to a document or to a derivation written beside it; any
   subtraction of the other side's own figures that answers a purpose is written out.
6. A filing-ready closing request is one paragraph, two tiers, item numbers only, each item's tier
   consistent with its stance. In the abbreviated form permitted by Step 5, verify instead that the
   final group conclusion states the dispositive proof limitation and the issuing party/date follow.
7. Nothing from the list in `references/filing-baseline.md` section 3 is present.
8. Header and sign-off label each party with the procedural status the instruction and the
   materials use, with no combined label of your own; every field the inputs state is filled in
   from them.
9. You read all four references before drafting.
10. Only the requested filing remains in the delivery directory. Render or extract any preview in a temporary working directory outside the delivery directory, and do not ship a PDF, image, text extract or alternate draft unless the instruction requests it.

## Failure modes

- **Blanket denial.** Denying everything invites the tribunal to discount the whole opinion. Deny
  what you have a ground to deny.
- **Label without reason.** "No objection to genuineness" alone is not an opinion.
- **Non answer on genuineness.** "The content cannot be confirmed" in place of a stance on
  genuineness leaves the property undecided.
- **Merits in a procedural posture.** When the evidence supports a procedural objection, answer
  each ground of the objection with the rule that decides it; name a point that goes only to price,
  quality or performance as a merits point in one sentence and do not argue it.
- **Recommending procedure.** Asking for originals, explanations or appraisal instead of stating
  what the defect stops the item from proving.
