# Limits that attach to common types of evidence

Apply this when you have to say what a particular item of the other side's evidence can and cannot
establish. Each entry gives the limit, why it holds generally, and the operation that turns it into
a written opinion. None of these limits is a conclusion by itself; read the item and find what it
actually attests before the limit tells you anything. The stance on genuineness is decided by the
origin classes in `cross-examination-method.md` section 3; these entries decide probative value.

## Reports: testing, inspection, valuation, audit, appraisal

An institution applies a stated method to a stated object and gives an opinion within that scope.

- **Limit.** The report proves what it attests, under the method it applied. It does not prove a
  proposition outside its attestation object, however close the subject matter looks.
- **Why it holds generally.** The scope statement is part of the report, because the institution's
  liability is bounded by it. A report made to answer one question carries no professional
  judgement on another.
- **Operation.** Find, in the report, the object attested, the standard applied, and the period or
  sample covered. Compare them with the fact in issue. If they differ, say so as a mismatch of the
  object of proof, and say what kind of record would bear on the fact in issue.
- **Also check.** Who commissioned it and who chose what it was given to examine; whether the
  institution and its personnel held the required qualification; whether it is a determination
  made in the proceedings or a report one party obtained by itself. Comment on sampling or on who
  witnessed the work only where the report or the inputs record how samples were taken or who was
  present; if they say nothing, do not assert that anyone did or did not take part.
- **A report the other side commissioned itself.** Accept that it was issued by the named
  institution, and put the weight of the opinion on why it cannot prove the fact it is offered
  for. Where it conflicts with a determination made in the proceedings, say which prevails and why.

## Documents one party made alone

Statements of account, demand and notice letters, internal ledgers, meeting notes, progress
records, photographs and messages prepared by one side.

- **Limit.** A party's own record does not by itself establish a fact adverse to the other party.
- **Why it holds generally.** Nothing in the document constrains its maker, so it proves the making,
  not the truth of the content.
- **Operation.** Look for what would convert it: a counter signature, an acknowledgement, a reply,
  a matching third party or public record. If none exists, decide genuineness by origin (a document
  the other side sent and your side received is genuine; an internal record nobody outside can
  check is not accepted) and then say that it proves at most that its maker made the assertion.
- **Counterexample.** A unilateral document is still good evidence against its own maker. Rely on
  the statements in it that go against its maker as that party's own admissions.

## Copies, screenshots, electronic records, recordings

- **Limit.** Without the original, or the original carrier for an electronic record, the item
  establishes at most that a document of that content was presented.
- **Why it holds generally.** Integrity and provenance cannot be checked on a derivative, and content
  that identifies neither its participants nor its time proves nothing about who did what when.
- **Operation.** Where no original or carrier has been produced, say that genuineness cannot be
  confirmed and name the specific fact the item therefore cannot carry. Check whether the
  participants and the time are identified and whether the record is complete or excerpted.
- **Counterexample.** Do not deny copies as a class when the content is not in real dispute or the
  copy favours you.

## Official and administrative records

Registers kept by public bodies, investigation records, administrative decisions, notarised
instruments.

- **Limit and strength.** These carry higher weight on the facts they record, and rebutting one
  takes contrary evidence of comparable weight.
- **Operation.** Distinguish what the body itself found or recorded from what it was told by a
  party, and confine the record to the former. Where it supports you, say so.

## Forms and slips offered in place of a required instrument

Submission forms, site confirmation slips, handover receipts, internal approvals offered to prove a
change, an authorisation, or a variation.

- **Limit.** Where the governing rule or the agreement requires a particular instrument, or a
  decision by a particular person or organ, a subordinate document of a different kind does not
  substitute for it.
- **Why it holds generally.** The formality identifies who assumed the obligation; a form signed by
  someone without that authority records an event, not a commitment.
- **Operation.** State the requirement first, then show which required element the bundle lacks,
  then state the consequence for the fact the other side wants to prove.

## Prior judgments, rulings, and other proceedings

- **Limit.** A decision binds on what it decided, between the parties it decided it for. A ruling
  that disposes of a case on a procedural ground decides nothing about the substantive rights. A
  decision in another case does not prove the facts in dispute in this one.
- **Operation.** Write the limit first, as a plain statement under the item: this decision cannot
  prove the named fact in this case, because it was made between other parties, on another basis,
  or without deciding that fact. Then, where the other side argues that the matter is already
  decided or is being relitigated, compare the two proceedings on the parties, the capacity in
  which each claimant sued, the legal basis of each claim, and the subject matter, and check
  whether the fact relied on here arose after the earlier decision.
- **Counterexample.** If you quote a favourable passage from the decision, you have accepted the
  document; attack its relevance, not its genuineness. Check whether the passage is the tribunal's
  own holding or its record of what a party argued, and attribute it accordingly.

## Contract and agreement texts

- **Limit.** The text proves what was agreed. It does not prove that a condition in it was satisfied,
  that a step under it was performed, or that a figure calculated under it is correct.
- **Operation.** Separate the clause from the event. If the other side offers the text to prove an
  event, say that the event needs its own record. Read the clause relied on together with any
  clause that qualifies it, and check on whom the obligation falls.
- **Who is bound.** A term binds those who agreed to it. Before accepting that a term reaches the
  party it is invoked against, check whether that party signed it, and, if the party's position
  came from someone else, whether it took that position subject to the term or objected when it
  took it. The person who did not agree and the consequence for the term belong in one passage.
- **Interpretation against the drafter.** Raise it only when the meaning of the clause is itself in
  issue at the current stage of the proceedings.

## Registration, formation, and capacity records

Business registers, capital contribution records, constitutional documents, licences, and reports
that verify such facts.

- **Limit.** A record made at a point in time proves the state of affairs at that point. It does not
  prove a continuing state of affairs over the period in dispute, and a status recorded in it does
  not prove how a party conducted itself during that period.
- **Operation.** Where the other side offers a formation or registration fact to establish something
  that is by nature ongoing, name the substitution and say what kind of record would show the
  ongoing state.
- **Registered object and transaction object.** Compare every identifier that connects the
  registration to the disputed property: account number, contract number, project, debtor,
  currency and period. A masked, truncated or visibly different identifier does not establish
  identity merely because the surrounding narrative treats the records as related.
- **Schedules and ambiguous fields.** A registration certificate that incorporates schedules or
  attachments proves only the scope visible without them. If an operative contract number, value
  or property description is illegible or internally inconsistent with the transaction at issue,
  state the narrower proposition the readable certificate proves; do not silently repair the
  field or expand its scope.

## Using these entries

- Apply only the entries that fit the item. An item can fall under two entries; an item that falls
  under none is decided on the general test: what it proves against what it is offered for.
- Every entry produces the same written shape: what the item establishes, where it stops, and the
  reason, with the rule of evidence or substantive law the limit rests on and its article number.

## Stop conditions

- These limits are defaults about a type, not findings about an item. If the item lacks the feature
  the entry assumes (an original was produced, the determination was made in the proceedings with
  both sides taking part), the entry does not apply.
- Do not stack entries to manufacture severity. One limit that bites is worth more than three that
  half fit.
- Where the inputs do not show the feature the entry turns on, confine the opinion to what the item
  shows on its face, and do not ask for further material in the filing.
