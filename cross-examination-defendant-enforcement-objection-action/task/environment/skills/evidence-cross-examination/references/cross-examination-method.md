# Cross examination method

Apply this whenever the deliverable is an opinion on evidence the opposing party produced. It
assumes you have built the evidence, ground and figure inventories of the entry point.

## 1. Section order

1. For a filing-ready opinion, use the header in `filing-baseline.md` section 5: title, case number, addressee tribunal, then a line
   for your client as the party giving the opinion and a line for the party whose evidence is
   examined, each labelled with the procedural status the instruction and the materials use for it,
   such as `原告：××` and `被告：××` (reverse them when you act for the defendant). Where the other
   side's evidence supports an application or objection, its capacity as applicant or objector may
   be added after that status.
2. In that form, use a preamble naming whose evidence is examined, filed with what, and how many items.
3. Item opinions, in the other side's order and numbering. One heading per item, or per group where
   the other side itself grouped its evidence, identifying the item by its number and name. Do not
   reorder the items by type or by importance.
4. In that form, add one short closing request after the last item opinion (section 9).
5. Use the filing-ready sign-off when its fields are supplied. For the abbreviated form allowed by
   the baseline, the identified issuing party and date suffice after the final group conclusion.

There are no other sections: no restated proof-purpose list or gratuitous duplicate index, no chapter regrouping the items by fact, no
chapter of favourable facts, no chapter of procedural demands, no chapter titled conclusions and
requests, and no merits argument after the items.

## 2. What every item opinion contains

Every item gets four things, each with its reason:

| Property | What you are deciding | Typical grounds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Genuineness | Whether the document is what it claims to be | Signatures and seals, who issued it, whether your side received or acknowledged it, whether an original or carrier exists |
| Lawfulness | Whether its source, method of obtaining and maker's qualification are proper | How it was obtained, whether the maker held the required qualification, whether a mandatory form was followed |
| Relevance | Whether it bears on an element actually in issue | Which element it touches or fails to touch, the period or subject it concerns |
| Probative value against the stated purpose | Whether it reaches the proposition its producer wants | Section 4 and `probative-value-limits.md` |

Rules for writing them:

- Each disputed stance and its reason are in the same sentence. "Genuineness is accepted because the letter
  bears the respondent's seal and was received by our side" is an opinion; "no objection to
  genuineness" is a label.
- Two properties that rest on the same ground may share one sentence, provided the ground is in
  it. Relevance is always decided for the item itself, because two items of the same kind can bear
  on different elements.
- Where consecutive official instruments share the same public origin, stance and reason, you may
  state uncontested genuineness/lawfulness once for the group. Other grouped properties are valid
  only when every named item rests on the same ground. Never use one blanket stance for unlike items.
- An item the other side listed but did not actually produce (no pages, no attachment, no carrier)
  still gets a stance: genuineness is not accepted because nothing was produced, and it proves
  nothing.
- Where a property is uncontroversial, the reason can be a few words. It cannot be missing.

## 3. Decide genuineness from the document's origin

Classify every item before writing it. The class decides the stance and, later, the tier in the
closing request.

| Origin class | Stance on genuineness | Where the attack goes | Request tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Signed by both sides, or made by a third party or a public body, nothing on its face to contest | Accepted, with the signatures or the issuing body as the reason | Relevance and probative value | Second tier only if it fails its purpose |
| Issued by the other side and sent to or received by your side (a letter, notice, statement it served), origin not in doubt | Accepted, because it is the other side's own document your side received | Purpose: it proves that the other side made the assertion, not that the assertion is true | Second tier |
| The other side's own internal record (logs, lists, photographs, messages, calculations, ledgers) with no signature, acknowledgement, reply or independent record from your side | Not accepted, or cannot be confirmed, with the reason (your side never saw, signed or acknowledged it and nothing independent records it) | Also say what it therefore cannot prove | First tier |
| A report the other side commissioned itself (testing, inspection, valuation, audit) | The report's existence and its issuing institution are accepted | Why it cannot prove the fact it is offered for: the object it examined, the method, the stated scope, who chose what it examined | Second tier |
| A copy, screenshot or electronic record with no original or carrier produced | Cannot be confirmed without the original, and name the fact it therefore cannot carry | Do not ask for production | First tier |
| Listed but not produced | Not accepted, nothing was produced | None needed | First tier |

Accepting genuineness never accepts the truth of what the document asserts, nor the purpose it is
offered for. Do not use "the content cannot be confirmed" in place of a stance on genuineness: say
whether genuineness is accepted, and then attack the content through the purpose.

Counterexample: do not contest genuineness to appear thorough. If the other side's document
favours you and nothing puts its origin in doubt, accepting it is what lets you rely on it.

## 4. The standard move: defeat the purpose

Write the gap explicitly: the document attests A, under the method or standard its maker applied;
the fact in issue is B; A does not reach B, because B depends on something the document neither
examined nor recorded. Name what the document does establish before saying where it stops.
"One sided" or "not objective" is not this move.

When the other side's purpose has several limbs, answer each limb. A limb that is not an element of
any claim or defence in issue is answered by saying it is not in issue; do not concede it as
established.

## 5. Test the chain

Group the items by the fact each is offered to prove. For each fact, list what the governing rule
requires before the fact can be found and check which requirements the bundle covers.

- Where the rule requires a particular instrument, authorisation or formality, a subordinate
  document does not stand in for it, and the missing one is the break point.
- Where a record should exist in the ordinary course and the bundle lacks it, the absence is itself
  an argument.

State the break once, under the item whose gap breaks the chain or under the last item of the
group that carries the fact: this chain does not close, because the element that would close it is
missing.

## 6. Favourable facts go under the item they come from

- Write the favourable fact under the item it comes from, in its own sentence after the criticism,
  saying what it proves for your client.
- A statement against its maker in a document the other side made alone still binds that party.
  You can rely on the statement as its maker's admission while not accepting the rest of the
  document; say so in those terms so the stance stays single.
- A figure or finding in the other side's documents that favours your client is adopted, not
  disputed. Do not attack the genuineness of a document and then rely on its content.
- Where your own evidence covers the same point, say the two corroborate each other.

Counterexample: do not manufacture an admission out of a party's argument, or out of what someone
told an investigator.

## 7. Burden and standing on the other side's assertions

- When the other side asserts a fact that would defeat or reduce your claim (invalidity, a defence,
  a set off, a condition not met, a bar to jurisdiction or to the forum), it carries the burden of
  proving that fact. Under the item offered for the assertion, say so with the rule on burden, and
  say what the evidence fails to show. "Nothing has been produced for this" is worth stating where
  it is true.
- When the other side invokes a right or a remedy, check whether the rule gives that right to the
  other side at all. If the right belongs to someone else (a creditor, a third party, a public
  body), say that the other side is not the holder, with the rule.
- When the other side says a transaction is invalid because some element is missing, check whether
  the governing rule actually makes that element a condition of validity. If it does not, say so
  with the rule before addressing whether the element is present.

## 8. When the evidence supports a procedural objection

A procedural objection (jurisdiction, arbitration agreement, forum, prior proceedings, standing)
is decided on procedural rules. Work it ground by ground.

- Every ground in the ground inventory is answered under the item offered for it. A ground the
  objection states in its text but supports with no item is answered under the item closest to it.
- For each ground, the answer carries its legal rule and all its limbs in one passage, under one
  item: the fact, the rule, and what follows. Do not split one ground across two items or two
  headings.
- Against a prior case or proceeding, set the two side by side: the parties, the capacity in which
  each claimant sued, the legal basis of each claim (name the rule each rests on), and the subject
  matter; then check whether the fact relied on here arose after the earlier decision.
- A point that goes only to price, quality or performance is named as a merits point in one
  sentence and not argued.

## 9. Closing request

For a filing-ready opinion, use one short paragraph after the last item opinion, with no heading of its own such as conclusions and
requests. It asks for the treatment of the evidence, listing item numbers in two tiers only:

- items the tribunal should not admit at all: the items whose genuineness or lawfulness is not
  accepted or cannot be confirmed (first tier in section 3), listed by number with the class reason
  in a phrase;
- items that should not be relied on for the purpose offered, or of which a named part should not
  be relied on: the items accepted as genuine that do not reach their purpose, including a report
  whose existence is accepted (second tier), listed by number with the purpose or part named.

Items that are accepted and do reach their purpose, or that help your client, are not listed. When
the evidence was filed to support an application or objection, the paragraph ends by asking that
the application or objection be rejected. Nothing else: no procedural application, and no request
that a claim be upheld or dismissed, that a defence not be supported, or that a sum be paid.

Verification: each listed item has an opinion above whose stance matches its tier; no item appears
in both tiers; no opinion above contradicts the request; the request is a single paragraph.

## 10. Stop conditions

- Do not comment on your own side's evidence here, and never write in the other side's voice.
- If an item's numbering or naming in the bundle is inconsistent, keep the other side's numbering
  and identify the document by number and name; do not add a note about the inconsistency.
- Do not contest a property you have no ground to contest.
- If the inputs do not show whether an original exists or who took part in a test, confine the
  opinion to what the item shows on its face.
