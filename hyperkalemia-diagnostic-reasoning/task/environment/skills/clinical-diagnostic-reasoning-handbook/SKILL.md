# Clinical Diagnostic Reasoning Records

Build concise, editable case records that keep source facts, interpretation, and unresolved questions distinct.

## Establish the reusable structure

Create the blank template before filling any case. Give it enough structure to hold:

- source observations with stable identifiers and locations;
- separate assessments of diagnosis, severity, and urgency;
- links from each assessment to supporting and limiting evidence;
- unresolved questions and the purpose of each proposed follow-up action.

Keep the template neutral. Do not prefill patient facts, expected conclusions, or task-specific examples. Use compact tables and short headings rather than instructional prose.

## Capture the source record

Inventory every readable report, panel, and repeated measurement before interpreting them. Treat one independently locatable observation as one record.

Transcribe the displayed value, unit, reference interval, date or time, source location, and reading uncertainty together. Recheck every character that affects a material conclusion. Preserve the precision and meaning actually shown by the source.

Distinguish missing, unreadable, not reported, negative, and within-range states. Never replace one state with another. Keep a source-side diagnosis or comment labeled as a reported statement rather than an observation made by the executor.

Reconcile the inventory after transcription. Account for every visible result and explain any item that cannot be read reliably.

## Form bounded assessments

Evaluate diagnosis, severity, and urgency as separate propositions. For each proposition, record its status, decisive evidence, applicable rule or threshold, certainty, and practical implication.

State what minimum evidence would be needed before adopting a conclusion. If that evidence is absent, preserve the diagnostic boundary instead of substituting related abnormalities. Do not treat missing evidence as evidence of absence.

Keep confirmation of a reported result, immediate risk assessment, and causal investigation as distinct workstreams. A need to confirm a result does not by itself remove the need to respond to a potentially dangerous reported value.

Use accompanying findings only for the question they can support. Do not infer an unreported history, examination finding, electrocardiogram, specimen condition, treatment, or unique cause.

## Handle repeated and time-linked evidence

Retain each measurement with its own source and time context. Before comparing records, establish whether they belong to the same patient, whether their timing is reliable, and whether they represent distinct specimens or observations.

Describe an observed difference without upgrading it to a clinical trajectory, treatment effect, or cause unless the source establishes the required relationship. Repeated values can strengthen confidence, but shared collection, labeling, transport, or testing conditions can limit their independence.

When an upstream fact or relationship changes, revise every dependent assessment. Do not leave conflicting final conclusions in different parts of the document.

## Close unresolved questions

For each unresolved issue, specify the question, the action that could resolve it, why the action matters, and which assessment could change. Separate recommended actions from actions already documented in the source.

## Produce and check the documents

Use the same blank-template structure for every case and keep cases in separate files. After completing each document:

1. Trace every final assessment back to source observations.
2. Trace every decisive observation forward to the assessments it affects.
3. Check that unknown, conditional, and confirmed states remain consistent.
4. Confirm that all visible source items are accounted for without duplication.
5. Open the Word file and verify that its tables remain editable and readable.
