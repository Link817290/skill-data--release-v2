# Evidence and Chronology

An employee dossier is a collection of observations, not a single current truth.
Join records by employee identifier and event, then preserve their individual
authority and timing. A later entry can correct a clerical record without proving
that a different task was actually performed. A planned assignment can exist
without attendance, and attendance can occur under a different assignment.
Use independent operative records to resolve those differences.

For each contested event, write a compact evidence chain:

- What the applicable rule requires, including any qualifying condition.
- Which source establishes the employee's relevant condition at that event.
- Which records describe a plan and which establish actual performance.
- Whether communications confirm execution, record a proposal or request approval.
- What remains unestablished and which decision depends on it.

Record the scope of every observation. A sample can support a sampled date;
extending it to a whole period requires evidence of coverage. Missing entries
call for reconstruction or an unresolved finding. Do not replace that finding
with an assumed entitlement denial or an assumed compliant practice.

For a date calculation, identify the triggering event and rule version before
computing the interval. State whether the starting day counts, whether the unit
is calendar or working days, what evidence supports additional days, and what
the next date means. Compute the end independently and compare it to the dates
being audited. An administrative reminder or a calculated boundary is a prompt
for procedural review; its legal effect depends on the governing rule and facts.

For wage entries, trace each entry to its cause, source period, authority and posted
status. Reconcile the arithmetic of listed components to recorded payment.
Describe an unexplained difference as a difference until its origin is proven.
Identify which corrections can be adopted now and which final calculations
require a receipt, approval or other missing evidence.
