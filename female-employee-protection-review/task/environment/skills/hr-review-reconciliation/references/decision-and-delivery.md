# Decision and Delivery Checks

A review should support a decision and subsequent execution. Separate its
established facts, adopted conclusions, proposed controls and open evidence
requests. For each substantive finding retain the employee, event scope,
applicable rule, operative evidence and reasoning. Support connected conclusions
with explicit cross-references rather than making a reviewer guess which wage
record belongs to a work or leave finding.

Use a common ledger when several deliverables represent the same investigation.
Keep identifiers consistent, but let human documents use clear business labels.
Organizing a settlement uncertainty under pay rather than leave is reasonable
provided the employee and affected decision remain clear. Avoid contradictory
values in summaries, tables and narrative paragraphs.

Actions need an accountable executor and a verifiable result. Specify the change,
the responsible role, timing and the evidence that will permit closure. Examples
of evidence categories include corrected system records, preserved approval
history, reconciliation statements, payment confirmation and written responses.
Select the category from the actual action and source workflow; do not claim it
already exists merely because it has been requested.

Before submission, perform a focused audit:

- Every employee and material issue in scope is either resolved or explicitly open.
- Every conclusion can be followed from its facts and operative rule.
- Date boundaries and transaction arithmetic have been independently checked.
- Each amount retains its item identity and stated degree of certainty.
- Every action links to a finding, has an owner and deadline, and defines closure.
- The report and all tables contain the same adopted propositions.
- All files reopen, references resolve, and source materials remain unchanged.

Keep uncertainty visible in the approval recommendation. An approved plan still
requires execution; a completed correction needs its own evidence. If a decisive
source is missing, give the interim control and follow-up route rather than an
invented final result.
