# Action and closure

For each material gap, specify the evidence needed and an accountable business role, without pretending that an assignment or deadline already exists. Examples include a warehouse count, shipment/return records, supplier identification confirmation, downstream receipts, and disposition certificates.

Separate proposed controls from completed facts. Mark unresolved tasks as pending until records establish completion. A practical action note can recommend temporary isolation for uncertain identity while requesting the relevant label or purchase evidence. Do not invent a completed destruction quantity or customer confirmation.

In the final note, lead with the supported disposition, describe the scope changes that matter for execution, and list the remaining blockers. Avoid a long generic risk checklist. If the evidence supports several operational implementations, accept them when they preserve the same constraints and uncertainty.

Check conclusions and action scope against the evidence, then independently reconcile counts, identifiers, intervals and relations. If one source error affects several tables, correct its origin and regenerate those tables before revisiting the narrative.

For a communication to operational recipients, make the triggering risk, scope, onward recipients, response route and close-out evidence explicit. An identification register proves membership, not quantity balance. Reconcile quantities to receipts, dispatches, returns and disposition evidence using consistent units and an observation date. Distinguish a public amendment date from an earlier operational notification date.
