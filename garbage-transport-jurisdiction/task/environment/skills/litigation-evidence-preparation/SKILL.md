# Litigation Evidence Preparation

Use this method when preparing civil filing documents from transaction records, a filing review response, and evidence held by third parties.

Read [jurisdiction-analysis.md](references/jurisdiction-analysis.md) when the intake label, the transaction description, and the proposed court do not immediately align.
Read [evidence-requests.md](references/evidence-requests.md) before drafting requests to different record holders.

Keep an internal distinction between a party's account, a contemporaneous record, the legal inference being advanced, and a fact that still needs proof. This distinction controls what the filing can assert and what a third party must be asked to verify.

For each proposed legal conclusion, connect the relevant transaction fact to an applicable rule and then to the requested procedural action. A quoted rule alone does not establish that its conditions hold in this case. An administrative intake label is evidence of what the system recorded, not a substitute for analyzing the parties' actual obligations.

Keep the court document and each record-holder request separate. An evidence request should identify the actual custodian, the event scope, the records sought, and the proposition those records could establish. It should not announce that documents not yet obtained have already proved the case.

Before finalizing, compare parties, locations, dates, vehicle or contract identifiers, and procedural states across the documents. Do not merge distinct sites into one judicial district merely because they share a city. Leave an unknown company identity or missing destination address unresolved rather than inventing one.
