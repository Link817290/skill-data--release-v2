# Request evidence from the actual record holder

## Trigger

Use this method when documents, access logs, service records, or witness information are held by an organization that is not a party to the dispute. Create a separate scope for each organization and site. Copying the same address and event period into every request can make an otherwise detailed letter unusable.

## Build the proof chain

Start with the proposition that needs support. To establish who commissioned work at a site, relevant records may include service agreements, outsourcing documents, or instructions involving the opposing party. To establish actual attendance or performance, dated operational ledgers can be compared with entrance records, access logs, surveillance excerpts, handover records, or contemporaneous photographs. To connect the work to the payment relationship, settlement statements, invoices, payment records, or reconciliation documents may be useful.

Ask the custodian for records within a discoverable event scope. Bind the site, date or period, relevant identifier, and type of activity in the same request. If different identifiers appear in different sources, preserve them as distinct items to be checked. Do not silently replace a handwritten driver name with the claimant's name, and do not assume that multiple photographs show multiple billable journeys.

Use independent records to corroborate a proposition rather than treating repeated copies of one record as independent proof. A ledger and a gate register can support different parts of the same event, while a settlement document addresses a separate payment link. A request for a knowledgeable employee's statement or contact information can identify who is able to explain the records. Do not invent that person's identity or claim that the organization has already confirmed the event.

## Practical delivery and legal boundaries

Identify the requester and the intended recipient sufficiently for the organization to route the letter. An unknown legal company name may remain a clear placeholder while the known site identifies the intended custodian. Ask for legible copies or an opportunity to inspect and copy records, and state how the response can be provided. Distinguish a requested response period from a legally compulsory deadline.

Explain that the material is requested for the identified dispute and limit disclosure to the litigation purpose. When records cannot be provided directly because they are unavailable, confidential, or outside the recipient's authority, request an explanation and consider an appropriate application for authorized evidence collection. An application for a lawyer's investigation order or court investigation is a future procedural route, not a claim that such an order already exists.

A private requester cannot create an automatic fine or detention by writing a letter. Any reference to compulsory cooperation must be conditional on the relevant lawful process and authority. Keep this distinction clear even when the request is firm.

## Verification

Check each letter against its own custodian and event. Trace every requested record to a useful evidentiary proposition. Confirm that the letter differentiates what the record might prove from what is already known. Finally, compare the recipient, district, date, identifiers, and purpose across letters so that a copied paragraph does not misdirect the request.
