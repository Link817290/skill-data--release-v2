# Analyze the transaction before selecting the jurisdiction argument

## Trigger

Use this analysis when a court intake message applies a general residence rule or carries a dispute label that may not describe the transaction. Separate what the message actually decided from what the applicant would like the court to decide next.

## Transaction and rule

Identify who arranged the work, who supplied the principal equipment, what performance was promised, and how payment was measured. Supplying a vehicle to move goods for a charge may support a transport-contract analysis. A wage label, a driver identity, or a single screenshot does not by itself decide whether the relationship is employment, transport, or another service. Explain why the adopted characterization fits the reported obligations. Treat a party's account of the agreement as an allegation until the records establish it.

The absence of a signed written agreement does not by itself settle whether an enforceable contractual relationship exists. Look for communications, requested performance, records of completed work, and the agreed basis of payment. Conversely, do not infer an agreed rate, a completed journey, or a particular debtor merely because a vehicle was photographed.

For a transport-contract jurisdiction argument, consider the legally recognized connecting places, including the origin, destination, and defendant's domicile. Bind each proposed place to the particular court's territorial jurisdiction. A destination somewhere in the same large city does not prove that it lies in a particular district. A clearly located origin can provide a different, independently supported connecting point.

Where more than one court is competent, explain the applicant's choice using a supported connection. The existence of a competent domicile court does not by itself eliminate other competent courts. This reasoning differs from asserting that every court in a city is competent.

## Procedural state and requested action

Read whether the record is an online intake review, a formal decision not to accept a case, a jurisdiction objection raised after service, or a transfer decision. Select language that responds to the actual recorded event. Do not state that a case has been accepted, an order has been issued, or a final jurisdiction ruling exists unless the materials establish it.

A request can seek reconsideration, correction of the intake treatment, or acceptance subject to lawful review without inventing a prior formal ruling. Distinguish the application being made from a guarantee of how the court must resolve it. Cite the substance of a rule accurately and avoid supplying a precise article number from uncertain memory.

## Verification

For each jurisdiction assertion, ask: which performance fact supports this place, where is that fact recorded, which territorial court does the place connect to, and what action follows? If one link is absent, qualify the assertion and seek the missing proof. Keep a strong supported origin argument separate from an unverified destination. Do not repair missing evidence by upgrading an allegation into a proven fact.
