# Decision allocation

## Roles

Use three roles and keep them distinct:

- **Draft**: the layer that prepares the decision. Drafting follows planning responsibility: the body that
  is accountable for the plan of that scope and carries the planning work drafts it. Check who runs the
  business: when a layer runs a business itself (it sets the operating plan and directs the operating
  company rather than holding it as an investment), that layer is the one that drafts, and an operating
  company without its own planning mandate supplies input. When the operating company holds the planning
  mandate and its own planning functions, it drafts and the layer above reviews or approves. If the settled
  work names who prepares plans, follow it.
- **Review**: a layer between draft and final that checks the draft against its own portfolio, targets
  and resources before it goes up. Add it only when that layer has something real to check.
- **Final**: the one layer that decides. Basis: one accountable decider per decision. Scope: when the
  settled work or a binding agreement (shareholder agreement, listing or regulatory rule) requires the
  consent of more than one body, name each consenting body and what happens if they disagree. "Informed",
  "filed" and "executes" are not roles in the grid; leave them out unless the inputs require them.

If you use a legend of short codes in tables, define it once and use each code for one role only.

## Matching the final approver to the control mode

Settled work usually gives each layer a control type (investment control, strategic control, operational
control) and sometimes a different mode for a specific industry. Use the mode that the layer holds over
the industry the decision is about. Basis: each mode names the lever the layer actually uses, capital
allocation, coordination of several industries, or running the business, and a layer approves what its
lever governs. Scope of the investment-control row: it applies where that control includes approving an
industry's entry, scale and direction; where the settled work defines pure financial control (return and
capital targets only), the layer approves capital allocation and targets and development strategy goes to
the layer below. Say which applies.

| Mode the layer holds over the industry | Development strategy of that industry | Competitive strategy of that industry | Unit-level competitive strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Investment control (the layer allocates capital across industries) | final approval; the industry does not decide its own development | not involved | not involved |
| Strategic control (the layer manages several industries and their synergy) | review when a higher layer holds investment control over the industry; final when there is no such layer or the industry has been delegated to it | final approval | not involved, except units the settled work escalates |
| Operational control (the layer runs the industry itself) | drafts it itself and decides it | final approval | usually delegated to the managing unit |
| Business unit that owns the industry (when the layer above does not run the industry itself) | drafts | drafts | final approval for the units it manages directly, except core units escalated below |

Check the table against the settled facts before applying it: when the inputs say a higher layer
delegates an industry "fully" to a lower layer, that lower layer takes the higher layer's column for that
industry and the higher layer drops out. When a specific settled statement names an approver that differs
from the table, the specific statement wins; say which one you followed.

## Portfolio decisions

Portfolio decisions set the size and pace of several industries or businesses relative to each other.
Basis: the mix is a capital allocation, so it sits with the body that holds the capital across them. Each
holder of that capital has its own portfolio decision: when a group holds main industries at the top and
delegates capital authority over a set of smaller businesses to a platform below, there are two portfolio
decisions, the mix of main industries (top layer) and the mix inside the delegated set (the platform). A
unit that analyses the set without holding its capital proposes; it does not decide the mix. When there is
no delegation, there is one portfolio decision.

## Sets of businesses managed together

When one unit manages a set of businesses as a whole, decide the set-level development and investment and
financing decisions in addition to each business's own competitive decisions. Allocate their roles with the
same tests as any other scope: the body with planning responsibility for the set drafts, and the holder of
capital authority over the set approves. Per-business directions do not replace a set-level decision when
the managing unit moves resources between the businesses; when it does not, say so and leave the row out.

## Investment and financing strategy versus investment approval

An investment and financing strategy is a plan (sources, uses, priorities) and follows the development
strategy of the same scope. Approval of individual investments and asset changes is a separate grid.
Basis: individual commitments are controlled by whoever holds the capital authority, in tiers
proportionate to their weight. For approvals:

- the platform board approves within its authority; the top layer approves what exceeds it by amount or
  nature, or what is strategically decisive for an industry it controls by investment;
- a body the settled work limits to review or execution (business units, virtual units, specialist
  departments) reviews and executes; it does not approve, unless the settled work grants it a tier;
- use the tier criteria the inputs give; do not invent numbers.

## Units that are treated differently

When the settled work names some units as core (run by senior executives, carrying large targets), ask
whether their competitive strategy should go one level higher than ordinary units. Basis: escalation is
justified when the higher layer carries a direct stake in those units, such as their targets or their
executives. If you escalate them, the managing unit reviews and the higher layer approves; ordinary units
stay with the managing unit. If the higher layer has no such stake, do not escalate.

A business that a platform manages directly, without a business unit in between, takes its strategy
decisions from that platform. Basis: decisions follow the management relationship, and a unit that manages
a different set of businesses has no mandate over it. If the settled work places the business under that
unit, the unit's column applies instead.

## Common allocation errors

- Letting the layer that runs an industry day to day also approve that industry's development strategy
  when a higher layer holds investment control over it.
- Pushing an industry-level competitive strategy up to a top layer that holds only investment control over
  that industry.
- Giving a virtual unit final authority because it holds the analysis.
- Treating a decision as covered because a principle sentence mentions it; a decision is covered only when
  the grid names its drafter and final approver.
