# Target and budget cascade

## Follow management relationships

Targets and budgets travel along management relationships, not along shareholdings. Basis: the body that
manages a unit is the one accountable for its results. Scope: when the settled work, listing rules or
minority-shareholder terms require the equity holder's governing body to set or approve a unit's targets or
budgets, the manager prepares and proposes, that body approves, and the chapter says so. A legal entity that
only exists for reporting is not a node; its units receive targets from whoever manages them. Before writing
the cascade, list every node that manages units and the units it manages, using the near-term structure the
settled work defines.

## One line per issuer

For each issuer, write exactly what it issues and to whom:

- The top layer issues to the nodes it manages directly, at the depth its control mode sets: aggregates for
  industries or platforms it controls by investment (for example one target set per main industry and one
  for a delegated set of smaller businesses), full operating targets for a business it runs itself. It does
  not issue to operating units whose management relationship has moved to a platform. State that boundary in
  one sentence, because it is the rule people get wrong when equity and management differ.
- A platform issues to each business unit it manages, to each business it manages directly without a
  business unit, and to headquarters departments if the inputs require.
- A business unit issues to every unit it manages, including units that senior executives run, because
  running a unit personally does not move the management relationship. Scope: when the settled work puts
  those units' targets in the higher layer's contract with the executive, the higher layer issues and the
  business unit tracks.
- A unit without departments of its own still issues to the units it manages. Basis: the issuing line
  follows the management relationship, and borrowing the platform's departments to prepare and track the
  targets does not move that relationship; do not bypass it by letting the platform board issue directly to
  those units. Scope: if the settled work assigns issuing for those units to another node, follow it and say
  who prepares and tracks.

Where the settled work allows temporary exceptions (a business under restructuring receiving targets
directly from a higher layer), state the exception with its condition, or leave it out; do not widen it.

## What goes with the cascade

Budgets follow the same lines as targets, because the manager that owns a target needs the budget that
delivers it. Scope: when the settled work gives authority over a type of budget (capital, operating,
headcount) to a different node, follow that authority for that type and state both lines. Strategy approval
and target issuing can sit at different layers: a unit whose strategy a platform approves can still receive
its targets from its business unit. Say so if both appear in your chapter, so the reader does not assume a
contradiction.

## Checks

1. Every node that manages units appears as an issuer once, or the reassignment the settled work makes is
   stated.
2. No issuer sends targets to a unit it does not manage.
3. The top layer's lines match its control mode for each receiver, and the equity-versus-management
   boundary is stated.
4. The cascade and the decision grid use the same node names.
