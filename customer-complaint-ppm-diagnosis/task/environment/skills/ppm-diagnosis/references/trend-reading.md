# Interpreting a complaint-rate series

Describe observed peaks, changes, product contributions and the adopted period.
Use recalculated rates with matching denominators, and distinguish absolute
changes from relative changes. A source inconsistency belongs in the diagnosis.

An observed movement does not establish a production, supplier or equipment
cause. A proposed investigation is distinct from an adopted causal explanation.
Statements that a cause is unknown or a proposed explanation is unsupported are
valid limitations, not causal claims.

Mention missing periods when explaining coverage, but do not report them as
observed results. Do not forecast from absent observations. Use outside benchmarks
only when they are within the task's authorized evidence.

Keep comparisons proportionate to the information available: complaint severity,
duplicate-counting rules and sales-cohort alignment may be unknown. State how an
unknown limits the conclusion instead of filling it with an assumption.
